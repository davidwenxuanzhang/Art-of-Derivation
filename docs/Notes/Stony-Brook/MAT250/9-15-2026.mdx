## Sum and Product

::::tip Sum and Product

Properties of the operation "sum" and "product" of real number

$$
\begin{align}
    &a+b=b+a \qquad \qquad \ \ \ \ \qquad ab=ba &\qquad \text{(Communicativity)}\\
    &(a+b)+c=a+(b+c) \qquad (ab)c=a(bc) &\qquad \text{(Associativity)}\\
    &a(b+c)=ab+ac \qquad \qquad\ \ \ (a+b)c=(ac+bc) &\qquad \text{(Distributivity)}\\
    & a+0=a=0+a\\
    & a 1=a=1 a\\
    & \forall a\in \mathbb{R}, \exists! x\in \mathbb{R}: a+x=0,\ x=-a\\
    & a\ne 0 \implies \exists! x\in \mathbb{R}: ax=1,\ x=a^{-1}=\frac{1}{a}

\end{align}
$$

Those are axioms, which means we just assume is true. 
:::info Example

Prove that if $a+x_1=a+x_2$, then $x_1=x_2$

$$
\begin{align*}
    x_1&=x_1+0 &\qquad \text{(Property 4)}\\
       &=x_1+\big(a+(-a) \big)  &\qquad \text{(Property 6)}\\
       &=(x_1+a)+(-a) &\qquad \text{(Property 2)}\\
       &=(a+x_1)+(-a) &\qquad \text{(Property 1)}\\
       &=(a+x_2)+(-a) &\qquad \text{(Given)}\\
       &=(x_2+a)+(-a) &\qquad \text{(Property 1)}\\
       &=x_2+\big(a+(-a)\big) &\qquad \text{(Property 2)}\\
       &=x_2+0 &\qquad \text{(Property 6)}\\
       &=x_2 &\qquad \text{(Property 4)} \tag*{$\blacksquare$}
\end{align*}
$$

## Order Axioms

::::tip Order Axioms
:::note 
For thsoe axioms, we are assuming they are true. In future, we can define real number and prove those axioms.
:::
1. Law of Trichotomy: Let $a,b\in \mathbb{R}$, the one and the only one of the following three possibilities is true:
$$
 a < b  \qquad a=b \qquad a>b
$$

<hr/>

2. For $a,b,c \in \mathbb{R}$: 

$$
a<b \iff a+c<b+c
$$
<hr/>

3. For $a,b,c \in \mathbb{R}$: 

$$
a<b
\implies
\begin{cases}
ac<bc &\quad c>0,\\
ac>bc &\quad c<0.
\end{cases}
$$

## Absolute Value

::::tip Absolute Value

For $a\in \mathbb{R}$,

$$
|a|\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}
\begin{cases}
a &\quad  \text{if }a\ge 0\\
-a &\quad \text{if } a<0
\end{cases}
$$

:::info Properties
For any real number $a$, $a\le |a|$

<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

Case 1: if $a \ge 0$, then $|a|=a$ by the definition of absolute value of $a$.

$$
\implies a\le |a|
$$

Case 2:  if $a < 0$, then $|a|=-a$ by the definition of absolute value of $a$.

$$
\begin{align*}
    a<0 \implies 2a&<2\cdot 0=0 &\qquad \text{(Order Axiom 3)}\\
                2a&<0\\
                (1+1)a&<0 \\
                a+a&<0   &\qquad\text{(Sum and Product Property 3)}\\
                a+a+(-a)&<0+(-a) &\qquad \text{(Order Axiom 2)}\\
                a+a+(-a)&<-a  &\qquad\text{(Sum and Product Property 4)}\\
                a+0&<-a  &\qquad\text{(Sum and Product Property 6)}\\
                \implies a&<-a=|a|\\
                \implies a&\le |a|
\end{align*}
$$
Therefore by two cases: 

$$
a\le |a| \tag*{$\blacksquare$}
$$
</details>
