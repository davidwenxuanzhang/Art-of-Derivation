## Sum and Product {#Sum-and-Product}

::::tip Sum and Product

Properties of the operation "sum" and "product" of real number

$$
\begin{align*}
    &a+b=b+a \qquad \qquad \ \ \ \ \qquad ab=ba &\qquad \text{(Communitativity)}\\
    &(a+b)+c=a+(b+c) \qquad (ab)c=a(bc) &\qquad \text{(Associativity)}\\
    &a(b+c)=ab+ac \qquad \qquad\ \ \ (a+b)c=(ac+bc) &\qquad \text{(Distributivity)}\\
    & a+0=a=0+a &\qquad \text{(Zero)}\\
    & a\cdot 1=a=1\cdot a &\qquad \text{(Unity)}\\
    & \forall a\in \mathbb{R}, \exists! x\in \mathbb{R}: a+x=0,\ x=-a &\qquad \text{(Subtraction)}\\
    & a\ne 0 \implies \exists! x\in \mathbb{R}: ax=1,\ x=a^{-1}=\frac{1}{a} &\qquad \text{(Division)}

\end{align*}
$$

Those are axioms, which means we just assume is true. 
:::info Example

Prove that if $a+x_1=a+x_2$, then $x_1=x_2$

$$
\begin{align*}
    x_1&=x_1+0 &\qquad \text{(Zero)}\\
       &=x_1+\big(a+(-a) \big)  &\qquad \text{(Subtraction)}\\
       &=(x_1+a)+(-a) &\qquad \text{(Associativity)}\\
       &=(a+x_1)+(-a) &\qquad \text{(Communitativity)}\\
       &=(a+x_2)+(-a) &\qquad \text{(Given)}\\
       &=(x_2+a)+(-a) &\qquad \text{(Communitativity)}\\
       &=x_2+\big(a+(-a)\big) &\qquad \text{(Associativity)}\\
       &=x_2+0 &\qquad \text{(Subtraction)}\\
       &=x_2 &\qquad \text{(Zero)} \tag*{$\blacksquare$}
\end{align*}
$$

:::
:::warning Practice
Prove that if $a\ne 0,\ ax_1=ax_2$, then $x_1=x_2$

<details>

\begin{align*}

       &=x_2\cdot \big(a\cdot\frac{1}{a}\big) &\qquad \text{(Associativity)}\\
       &=x_2\cdot 1 &\qquad \text{(Division)}\\
       &=x_2 &\qquad \text{(Unity)} \tag*{$\blacksquare$}
\end{align*}
$$

## Difference and Quotient

::::tip Difference and Quotient

For $a,b \in \mathbb{R}$:
$$
a-b \overset{\mathrm{def}}{=} a+(-b)
$$
For $b\ne 0$:

$$
\frac{a}{b}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}a\cdot \frac{1}{b} \text{ or } ab^{-1}
$$

:::warning Question

Prove the following:

1. $\forall a\in \mathbb{R}, a\cdot 0=0 \cdot a=0$

<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

$$
\begin{align*}
    a\cdot 0&= a\cdot (0+0) &\qquad \text{(Zero)}\\
    a\cdot 0 &=a\cdot 0+ a\cdot  0&\qquad \text{(Distributivity)}\\
\end{align*}
$$
Let $a\cdot 0=x$, then from $0$:
$$
\begin{align*}
    0&=x+(-x) &\qquad \text{(Subtraction)}\\
     &=x+x+(-x)  0&\qquad \text{(From above)}\\
     &=x+\big(x+(-x)\big) &\qquad \text{(Associativity)}\\
     &=x+0&\qquad \text{(Subtraction)}\\
     &=x &\qquad\text{(Zero)}
\end{align*}
$$
Therefore, with Associativity:
$$
a\cdot 0=0 \cdot a=0
$$


</details>
<hr/>

2. $(-a) b=-(ab)$


<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

From [Subtraction](#sum-and-product-sum-and-product):

$$
ab+\big(-(ab)\big)=0
$$

Additionally:

$$
ab+\big((-a)b\big)=\big(a+(-a)\big)b=0\cdot b=0
$$
Therefore, by previous practice:

$$
ab+\big(-(ab)\big)=ab+\big((-a)b\big)\implies (-a)b= -(ab)
$$

</details>
<hr/>


3. $(-a)(-b)=ab $

<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

From the previous practice, we know $(-a) b=-(ab)$, then:

$$
ab+\big(-(ab)\big)=ab+\big((-a) b\big)0
$$
And
$$
(-a)(-b)+\big(-(a)b\big)=-(a)(-b+b)=-a\cdot 0=0
$$
Therefore:

$$
(-a)(-b)+\big((-a) b\big)=ab+\big(-(a)b\big) \implies (-a)(-b)=ab
$$
</details>

:::

## Axioms for the Order Relation {#Order-Axioms}

::::tip Axioms for the Order Relation
:::note 
For thsoe axioms, we are assuming they are true. In future, we can define real number and prove those axioms.
:::
1. **Law of Trichotomy**: Let $a,b\in \mathbb{R}$, the one and the only one of the following three possibilities is true:
$$
 a < b  \qquad a=b \qquad a>b
$$

<hr/>

2. **Addition Law**: For $a,b,c \in \mathbb{R}$: 

$$
a<b \iff a+c<b+c
$$
<hr/>

3. **Multiplication law**: For $a,b,c \in \mathbb{R}$: 

$$
a<b
\implies
\begin{cases}
ac<bc &\quad c>0,\\
ac>bc &\quad c<0.
\end{cases}
$$

<hr/>

4. **Transitive Law**: For $a,b,c\in \mathbb{R}$:

$$
a<b \land b<c \implies a<c
$$

## Absolute Value

::::tip Absolute Value

For $a\in \mathbb{R}$,

$$ 
|a|\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}
\begin{cases}
a &\quad  \text{if }a\ge 0\\
-a &\quad \text{if } a<0
\end{cases}
$$

:::info Properties
For any real number $a$, $a\le |a|$

<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

Case 1: if $a \ge 0$, then $|a|=a$ by the definition of absolute value of $a$.

$$
\implies a\le |a|
$$

Case 2:  if $a < 0$, then $|a|=-a$ by the definition of absolute value of $a$.

$$
\begin{align*}
    a<0 \implies 2a&<2\cdot 0=0 &\qquad \text{(Multiplication law)}\\
                2a&<0\\
                (1+1)a&<0 \\
                a+a&<0   &\qquad\text{(Distributivity)}\\
                a+a+(-a)&<0+(-a) &\qquad \text{(Addition Law)}\\
                a+a+(-a)&<-a  &\qquad\text{(Zero)}\\
                a+0&<-a  &\qquad\text{(Subtraction)}\\
                \implies a&<-a=|a|\\
                \implies a&\le |a|
\end{align*}
$$
Therefore by two cases: 

$$
a\le |a| \tag*{$\blacksquare$}
$$
</details>

## Practice

:::warning Exercise

Prove that for real numbers $a$ and $b$, if $a\ne b$, then:

$$
a^2+b^2>2ab
$$

<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

The inequality can be written as:

$$
\begin{align*}
    a^2+b^2>2ab &\iff a^2+b^2+(-2ab)> 2ab+(-2ab) &\qquad \text{(Addition Law)}\\
                &\iff a^2+b^2-2ab> 0 &\qquad \text{(Subtraction)}\\
                &\iff (a-b)^2> 0 &\qquad \text{(Distributivity)}\\
\end{align*}
$$
Thus:
$$
\begin{align*}
    a\ne b &\iff a-b\ne 0\\
        &\implies (a-b)^2>0
\end{align*}
$$
By the previous result: 

$$
a^2+b^2>2ab \tag*{$\blacksquare$}
$$
</details>

:::
