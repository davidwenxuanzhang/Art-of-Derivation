### Complement

Fix a "universal" set $U$, then:

::::tip Complement

For $A\subseteq U$, the **complement** of $A$, denoted by $A^{c}$, is defined by:

$$
A^{c}=U\setminus A=\{x\in U \mid x\notin A\}
$$

:::info Example
Let $U=\mathbb{Z}$, and $E$ is the set of all even integers, then:

$$
E^{c}=\mathbb{Z}-E=\{n\in \mathbb{Z}\mid n\notin E\}=\{n\in \mathbb{Z} \mid 2 \nmid n\}=\text{ the set of odd integers}
$$

:::
:::danger Remark

The conclusion: "therefore, the complement of the set of all even integers is the set of all odd integers" is **WRONG** because it does not state the universal set $U$, without which the statement has no mathematical meaning.

:::

### Set Theorem

Recall that:

$$
A\cap B = \{x\mid x\in A \land x\in B\}\qquad A\cup B=\{x\mid x\in A \lor x\in B \}
$$

With the universal set $U$:

$$
A^{c}=\{x\in U\mid x\notin A \}
$$

and this leads to the set theorem

:::info Set Theorem
Let $A,B,C\subseteq U$, then

1. Associativity

$$
A\cup(B\cup C)=(A\cup B)\cup C, \qquad A\cap(B\cap C)=(A\cap B)\cap C 
$$

2. Commutativity

$$
A\cup B=B\cup A ,\qquad A\cap B=B\cap A
$$

3. Distributivity

$$
A\cup (B\cap C)=(A\cup B)\cap (A\cup C),\qquad A\cap (B\cup C)=(A\cap B)\cup (A\cap C)
$$

4. De Morgan's Law

$$
(A\cup B)^{c}=A^{c}\cap B^{c},\qquad (A\cap B)^{c}=A^{c}\cup B^{c}
$$

5. Complementation

$$
A\cup A^{c}=U,\qquad A\cap A^{c}=\emptyset
$$

6. Double Complement

$$
(A^{c})^{c}=A
$$
:::

### Cartesian Product

::::tip Cartesian Product

Given nonempty sets $X$ and $Y$, the **Cartesian product** of $X$ and $Y$ is:

$$
X\times Y=\{(x,y)\mid x\in X, y\in Y \}
$$

Its elements are ordered pairs, that is to say, two ordered pairs are precisely equal when their corresponding coordinates are equal:

$$
(x_1,y_1)=(x_2,y_2) \iff x_1=x_2 \text{ and } y_1=y_2
$$

Moreover, the ordered pair $(x,y)$ has first coordinate $x$ and second coordinate $y$; when $X=Y$, we say:

$$
X\times Y=X\times X=X^2
$$

:::info Example

Let $X=\{a,b,c\}$ and $Y=\{a,b\}$, then:

$$
\begin{align*}  
    X\times Y=\{(a,a), (a,b),(b,a),(b,b),(c,a),(c,b)\}\\
    Y\times X=\{(a,a), (a,b),(a,c),(b,a),(b,b),(b,c)\}
\end{align*}
$$

Because, for example, $(c,a)\in X\times Y$ but $(c,a)\notin Y\times X$, therefore:


$$
X\times Y\ne Y\times X
$$

### Propositions

::::info Propositions

For all sets $A$, $B$, $C$, and $D$:

1. 
$$
A\times (B\cup C)=(A\times B)\cup (A\times C)
$$

<hr/>

2. 

$$
A\times(B\cap C)=(A\times B)\cap (A\times C)
$$

<hr/>

3. 

$$
(A\times B)\cap (C\cup D)=(A\cap C)\times (B\cap D)
$$

<hr/>

4. 
$$
(A\times B)\cup (C\cup D)\subseteq (A\cup C)\times (B\cup D)
$$

:::danger Remark

Equality cannot hold in proposition 4. Let:
$$
A=\{a\},\qquad B=\{b\},\qquad C=\{c\},\qquad D=\{d\}
$$

Then: 

$$
(A\times B)\cup (C\times D)=\{(a,b),(c,d)\}
$$

whereas

$$
\begin{align*}
    (A\cup C)\times (B\cup D)&=\{a,c\}\times \{b,d\}\\
    &=\{(a,b),(a,d),(b,c),(b,d) \}
\end{align*}
$$
Therefore the inclusion is necessary.
:::
