Divisibility​

Divisibility

Given two integers aaa and bbb, we say that bbb divides aaa or aaa is a multiple of bbb to mean that there is an unique integer qqq such that a=bqa=bqa=bq

Why Unique

The reason that we say it is unique integer is because for the proposition "0 divides 00 \text{ divides }00 divides 0", it is true for all integers qqq, but obviously 000 cannot divides anything; therefore, unique is really important.

Notation:

b∣a:b divides ab \mid a : b \text{ divides } ab∣a:b divides a

Even Number and Odd Number​

Even Number

With the (definition), we define:

a is even if 2∣aa \text{ is even if } 2\mid aa is even if 2∣a
Odd Number

Similarly, with the (definition), we define:

a is odd (not even) if 2∤aa \text{ is odd (not even) if } 2\nmid aa is odd (not even) if 2∤a
Example

Prove 101101101 is an odd number.

Derivation
Warning

For the purpose of this class, you cannot just say 101÷2=50.5  ⟹  not even101\div 2=50.5\implies \text{not even}101÷2=50.5⟹not even. This will recieve a 0, and you have to use what we learn and prove it.

Let qqq be an integer

  • Case 1: q<50  ⟹  2q<100q < 50 \implies 2q<100q<50⟹2q<100 by Multiplication law
  • Case 2: q=50  ⟹  2q=100q = 50 \implies 2q=100 q=50⟹2q=100
  • Case 4: q>50  ⟹  q≥51  ⟹  2q≥102q > 50 \implies q \ge 51 \implies 2q \ge 102q>50⟹q≥51⟹2q≥102

Therefore: 101101101 is not even   ⟹  \implies ⟹ 101101101 is odd

■\blacksquare■

Proof by Contradiction​

Proof by Contradiction

If we want to prove PPP is true:

Start with assume ¬P\neg P¬P is true, then implies something always false, also known as contradiction (Q∧¬Q)(Q\land \neg Q)(Q∧¬Q)

Example

There do not exists integers m,nm,nm,n such that 2m+4n=1012m+4n=1012m+4n=101

Derivation

Suppose there are such integers mmm and nnn so that 2m+4n=101

  ⟹  2(m+2n)=101(Distributivity)  ⟹  2∣101  ⟹  101 is even\begin{align*} &\implies 2(m+2n)=101 &\qquad \text{(Distributivity)} \\ &\implies 2\mid 101 &\implies 101 \text{ is even} \end{align*}​⟹2(m+2n)=101⟹2∣101​(Distributivity)⟹101 is even​

However, from previous example, 101101101 is odd, and this gives us a contradiction.

Warning

Thus, there is no such integers.

Proof by Contrapositive​

Proof by Contrapositive

Recall that the contrapositive statement of P  ⟹  QP\implies QP⟹Q is:

¬Q  ⟹  ¬P\neg Q\implies \neg P ¬Q⟹¬P

More importantly:

P  ⟹  Q≡¬Q  ⟹  ¬P P\implies Q\equiv \neg Q\implies \neg PP⟹Q≡¬Q⟹¬P

Therefore, to prove the statement P  ⟹  QP\implies QP⟹Q, we can alternatively prove ¬Q  ⟹  ¬P\neg Q\implies \neg P¬Q⟹¬P

Example

Prove that if a≤ba\le ba≤b and a≥ba\ge ba≥b, then a=ba=ba=b

Derivation

Let P:a≤b∧a≥bP: a\le b \land a\ge bP:a≤b∧a≥b and Q:a=bQ:a=bQ:a=b

Then, ¬Q≡a≠b\neg Q\equiv a\ne b¬Q≡a=b and ¬P≡a<b∨a>b\neg P \equiv a<b \lor a>b¬P≡a<b∨a>b

Assume ¬P\neg P¬P which a≠ba\ne ba=b, then by Law of Trichotomy, either a>ba>ba>b nor a<ba<ba<b, which is ¬Q\neg Q¬Q.

Therefore, by contrapositive:

a≤b∧a≥b  ⟹  a=b■a\le b \land a\ge b \implies a=b \tag*{$\blacksquare$}a≤b∧a≥b⟹a=b■

Mathematical Induction​

Induction Principle

Let P(n)P(n)P(n) be a statement involving a general positive integers nnn, then P(n)P(n)P(n) is true for every positive nnn provided that:

  1. P(n)P(n)P(n) is true

  2. For every positive integer kkk, the implication:

    P(k)  ⟹  P(k+1)P(k) \implies P(k+1)P(k)⟹P(k+1)

    is true.

Natural Numbers Only
