## Consequences of the Euclidean Algorithm

Recall from last time about Bézout’s Identity (claude: give a link)

:::tip Bézout’s Identity

For $a,b\in \mathbb{Z}$, where $a$ and $b$ not both zero, if $\operatorname{gcd}(a,b)=d$, then exists $m,n\in \mathbb{Z}$ such that:

$$
am+bn=d
$$

:::

If $\operatorname{gcd}(a,b)=d$ is proposition $P$ and $\exists\ m,n \in \mathbb{Z}: am+bn=d$ is $Q$, then Bézout’s Identity gives $P \implies Q$.

However, its converse $Q\implies P$ is generally not true, and it is only true for $d=1$

::::tip Coprime

Two integers $a,b$, not both zero, are **Coprime** if $\operatorname{gcd}(a,b)=1$

:::danger Remark 

If $a$ and $b$ coprime, then exist integer $m$, $n$ such that:

$$
am+bn=1
$$

:::

::::



::::info Theorem

Two integers $a$ and $b$ are coprime if and only if there exist integers $m,n$ such that $am+bn=1$

$$
a\mid bc\implies a\mid c

## Equivalence Relations and Partitions


### Partition
 
::::tip Partition

Let $X$ be non-empty set. A **Partition** of $X$ is a collection $\Pi\subseteq \mathcal{P}(x)$ such that:

1. every member of $\Pi$ is non-empty
    $$
    A\in \Pi\implies A\ne \emptyset
    $$
2. distinct elements of $\Pi$ are disjoint
    $$
    A_1, A_2\in \Pi\text{ and }A_1\ne A_2 \implies A_1\cap A_2=\emptyset
    $$
3. the members of $\Pi$ covers $X$:
    $$
    \forall x\in X, \ \exists A\in \Pi\text{ such that }x\in A
    $$

### Relation

::::tip Relation

A **Relation** $R$ on a nonempty set $X$ is a subset of $X\times X=X^{2}$

$$
R\coloneqq \{(x,y): x,y\in X \}
$$

:::note Notation

$$
\begin{align*}
    &(x,y)\in R\iff xRy\ \iff x \sim y\\
    &(x,y)\notin R\iff x
    \not{R}y\ \iff x \nsim y
\end{align*}
$$

:::

:::warning Examples



:::

::::

::::tip Definitions

1. A relation $\sim$ on $X$ is **Reflexive** if

    $$
    \forall a\in X,\ a\sim a
    $$

2. A relation $\sim $ on $X$ is **Symmetric**  if 
    $$
    \forall a,b \in X,\ a\sim b\implies b\sim a
    $$

3. A relation $\sim$ on $X$ is **Transitive** if
    $$
    \forall a,b,c \in X, \ a\sim b\text{ and }b\sim c\implies a\sim c
    $$
4. An **Equivalence Relation** on $X$ is a relation that s reflexive, symmetric, and transitive

:::danger Condition

::::tip Congurence Modulo

Fix a positive integer $m$; for $x,y\in \mathbb{Z}$, define

$$
x\equiv y \pmod {m}\iff m\mid (x-y)
$$

and $x$ and $y$ are said to be **Congurent Modulo $m$**

:::info Proposition

Congurence modulo $m$ is an equivalence relation on $\mathbb{Z}$

:::tip

Let $\Pi$ be a partition on a non-empty set $X$, we define $\sim \Pi$ on $X$ as follows:

$$
a\sim \Pi b\iff a\text{ and }b \text{belongs to some member in } \Pi
$$

:::tip Equivalence Class

Let $\sim $ be an equivalence relation on a non-empty set $X$. For $a\in X$, the **Equivalence Class** of $a$ is:

$$
[a]=\{x\in X\mid x\sim a \} \subseteq X,\quad [a] \in \mathcal{P}(x)
$$

:::

:::tip Quotient Set

The set of all equivalence classes, also known as **Quotient Set**, is denoted by:

$$
X/ \sim =\{[a]\mid a\in X \}\subseteq \mathcal{P}(x)
$$

:::

:::info Theorem

Suppose $\sim $ is an equivalent relation on $X$, then

$$
[a]=[b] \text{ or } [a]\cap [b]=\emptyset
$$

:::
