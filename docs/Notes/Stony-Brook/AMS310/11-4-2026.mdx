## Hypothesis Tests
 
:::info Rough idea of Hypothesis Tests

- Hypothesis: $\theta=\theta_0$; we also call this $\mathbb{M}(\theta_0)$
- Observed data: $m$

- Target: $\mathbb{P}[a\le \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)\le b \mid C ],\quad m\in [a,b]$
    In this situation, $[a,b]$ is called the acceptance region: we accept the hypothesis if our observed data land in this interval.
    - This is the definition of a test
    - If $m\in [a,b]$, we accept the hypothesis $\theta=\theta_0$
    - If $m\notin [a,b]$, we reject the hypothesis:
    $$
    (-\infty ,a)\cup (b,\infty)\text{ is the rejection region}
    $$
Below is a better definition.

:::

::::tip Hypothesis Tests

A **Hypothesis Test** is a rule that tells us whether to accept or reject the hypothesis
- It will involve a test statistic, also known as the summary statistic of the data.
- $\mathbb{M}(\theta_0)$ is a model for uncertainty in the test statistic.

:::info Example

Back to our guessing game example where the class of people randomly guess 10 T/F questions and count the number of correct guesses.
- Test statistic: sample mean $m=\mathbb{X}$
- Hypothesis: $\mu=5,\sigma^2=\frac{5}{2}, N=100\rightarrow \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)\rightarrow \mathbb{X}\sim \mathcal{N}\left(\mu_0,\frac{\sigma^2}{N}\right)=\mathcal{N}(5,0.025)$
- Our test has the acceptance region $[4.6,5.2]$

2. Reject the hypothesis when it is true (false negative)

### Quantifying Error

:::tip Quantifying Error

- False Negative:

$$
\begin{align*}

\varepsilon_{-}&=\mathbb{P}[\text{reject }\theta=\theta_0\mid \theta=\theta_0\text{ is true} ]\\
&=1-\mathbb{P}[\text{accept }\theta=\theta_0\mid \theta=\theta_0\text{ is true} ]\\
&=1-\mathbb{P}[a\le \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)\le b\mid C]\\
&=1-F[b\mid \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)]+F[a\mid \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)]
\end{align*}
$$


- False Positive:

$$
\begin{align*}
\varepsilon_{+}&=\mathbb{P}[\text{accept }\theta=\theta_0\mid \theta=\theta_0\text{ is false} ]\\
&=\mathbb{P}[a\le \mathbb{M}(\theta_{\text{alt}})\le b\mid C]
\end{align*}
$$

    Notice that here we use $\theta_{\text{alt}}$ instead of $\theta_0$ because our hypothesis is false, which means we need an alternative model.

:::

:::info Example

Back to our guessing game where $\mu=5, N=100, \sigma^2=\frac{5}{2}$

$$
\mathbb{M}(\theta_0)\longrightarrow \mathbb{X}\sim \mathcal{N}\left(5,\frac{\frac{5}{2}}{100} \right)=\mathcal{N}(5,0.025)
$$

- Test: Acceptance region as $[4.75,5.25]$
- False negative rate:
    $$
    \begin{align*}
    \epsilon_{-}&=\mathbb{P}(\text{reject }\mu=5\mid \mu=5\text{ is true})\\
    &= 1- \mathbb{P}[4.75\le \mathbb{X}\le 5.25\mid \mathbb{X}\sim \mathcal{N}(5,0.025)]\\
    &=0.114
    \end{align*}
    $$
- False positive rate: since $\mu=5$ is wrong, we use an alternative model
    $$
    \longrightarrow \mathbb{X}\sim \mathcal{N}(5.1,0.02)
    $$
    $$
    \begin{align*}
    \epsilon_{+}&=\mathbb{P}(\text{accept }\mu=5\mid \mu=5\text{ is wrong})\\
    &=\mathbb{P}[4.75\le \mathbb{X}\le 5.25\mid \mathbb{X}\sim \mathcal{N}(5.1,0.02)]\\
    &=0.414
    \end{align*}
    $$

:::
