## Geometric Distribution

::::tip Geometric Distribution

Similar setup to the binomial, but instead of counting "success" in a fix number of trail, the **Geometric Distribution** counts the number of trails needed ot observe a "success".

- Outcomes: $1,2,3,\dots \infty$
    $$
    \mathbb{X}\sim \operatorname{geometric}(p)
    $$

- Probability mass function:  

    $$
    \begin{align*}
    M[x\mid \mathbb{X}]&= \mathbb{P}\left[\mathbb{X}=x\mid \mathbb{X}\sim \operatorname{geometric}(p) \right]\\
    &=(1-p)^{x-1}p
    \end{align*}
    $$
    
    Veryfy: $1=\sum_{x=1}^{\infty}M[x\mid \mathbb{X}]=\sum_{x=1}^{\infty}(1-p)^{x-1}p=\frac{p}{1-(1-p)}$

- Expectations:

    $$
    \mu =\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{geometric}(p)]=\sum_{x=1}^{\infty}xM[x\mid \mathbb{X}]=\sum_{x=1}^{\infty} x (1-p)^{x-1}p=\frac{1}{p}
    $$

- Variance

    $$
    \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}^2]=\sum_{x=1}^{\infty}x^2(1-p)^{x-1}p=\frac{2-p}{p^2}
    $$

    $$
    \sigma^2=\mathbb{E}\left[(\mathbb{X}-\mu_{\mathbb{X}})^2\right]=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}^2]-\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}]^2=\frac{2-p}{p^2}-\left( \frac{1}{p}\right)^2=\frac{1-p}{p^2}
    $$

:::info Example

How many cards, with replacement, are needed to draw a 9 from a well-shuffled 52 card deck?

$$
\mathbb{X}\sim \operatorname{geometric(p=\frac{1}{13})}\qquad \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}]=\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{13}}=13
$$
$$
\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{X}\ge 10 \mid \mathbb{X}\sim \operatorname{geometric}(p=\frac{1}{13}) \right)=\sum_{x=10}^{\infty}M[x\mid \mathbb{X}]=\left(\frac{12}{13}\right)^9\approx 0.99
$$


:::

## Poisson Distribution

:::tip Poisson Distribution

Discrete "success" to a continuous "frequency"

A **Poisson Distribution** counts the number of events that occur in a specified timeframe/intercal

- Assumptions: 
    1. The rate of event occurence (events per interval/timeframe) in constant. It is often known.
    2. The probability that two (or more) events happen simultaneously is negligible.
    3. The events are independent

- Result: 1 parameter: $\lambda$, the average number of events in the intercal/timeframe where $\lambda>0$

    $$
    \mathbb{X}\sim \operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)
    $$

- Probability mass function
    $$
    Mpx\mid \mathbb{X}=\mathbb{P}[\mathbb{X}=x\mid \mathbb{X}\sim \operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)]=\frac{\lambda^{x}e^{-\lambda}}{x!}
    $$
    Verify: $\sum_{x=1}^{\infty}M[x\mid \mathbb{X}]=1=\sum_{x=1}^{\infty} \frac{\lambda^{x}k}{x!}=k\sum_{x=1}^{\infty} \frac{\lambda^{x}}{x!}=ke^{\lambda}$

- Expectations

    $$
    \mu=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}]=\sum_{x=0}^{\infty}x\frac{\lambda^{x}e^{-\lambda}}{x!}=\lambda
    $$

- Variance

    $$
    \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}^2]=\lambda^2+\lambda
    $$
    $$
    \sigma^2=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}^2]-\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}]^2=\lambda^2+\lambda-\lambda^2=\lambda
    $$

## Hypergeometric Distribution

::::tip Hypergeometric Distribution

Similar to binomial (the number of "success" in $N$ trails) but no replacement

- Parameters: 
    - $N$: Number of total items/population
    - $S$: Total number of "successes" in the population
    - $n$: number of trails
    $$
    \mathbb{X}\sim \operatorname{hypergeometric}(N,S,n)
    $$

- Probability mass function:

    $$
    M[x\mid \mathbb{X}]=\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbb{X}=x\mid \mathbb{X}\sim \operatorname{hypergeometric}(N,S,n) \right]=\frac{\binom{S}{x}\binom{N-S}{n-x}}{\binom{N}{n}}
    $$

    Verify: $1=\sum_{x}M[x\mid \mathbb{X}]=\sum_{x}\frac{\binom{S}{x}\binom{N-S}{n-x}}{\binom{N}{n}}=\frac{\binom{N}{n}}{\binom{N}{n}}$

- Expectations:

    $$
    \mu=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}]=\sum_{x}x\frac{\binom{S}{x}\binom{N-S}{n-x}}{\binom{N}{n}}=\frac{nS}{N}
    $$

- Variance

    $$
    \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}(\mathbb{X}-1)]=\frac{S(S-1)n(n-1)}{N(N-1)} \implies \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}^2]=\frac{S(S-1)n(n-1)}{N(N-1)}+\frac{nS}{N}
    $$

    $$
    \sigma^2=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}^2]-\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}]^2=\frac{nS(N-S)(N-n)}{N^2(N-1)}
    $$
