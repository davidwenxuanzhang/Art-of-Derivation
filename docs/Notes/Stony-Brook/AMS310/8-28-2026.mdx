## Logic
Logic is the study of truth.

### Proposition

::::tip Proposition

Proposition is a statement that is either true or false but not both

:::info Example
1. Albany is the capital of New York 
    → A **True Proposition**

2. Today (8/28/2026) is Saturday  
    → A **False Proposition**

3. $2+3=5$  
    → A **True Proposition**
:::

::::

### Propositional Calculus

:::tip Propositional Calculus

Propositional Calculus gives operations and relations on propositions:

<div style={{ width: 'fit-content', margin: '0 auto' }}>
| Name | Notation | Notion |
|:---|:---|:-----------|
| Not/Negation | $\neg P$ | True if $P$ is false, and false if $P$ is true |
| And | $P\land Q$ | True if both $P$ and $Q$ are true, and false otherwise |
| Or | $P\lor Q$ | True if either $P$ or $Q$ is true, and false otherwise |
</div>

:::

### De Morgan's Law

:::tip De Morgan's Law
$$
\neg (P\land Q)\equiv \neg P \lor \neg Q \qquad \text{and}\qquad \neg (P\lor Q)\equiv \neg P \land \neg Q
$$

### Implication

::::tip Implication

Implication denoted as $P\rightarrow Q$, which means if $P$ then $Q$, or $Q$ is necessary for $P$, $Q$ whenever $P$, where $P$ is the antecedent and $Q$ is the consequant
<div style={{ width: 'fit-content', margin: '0 auto' }}>

| $P$ | $Q$ | $P \rightarrow Q$ |
|:---:|:---:|:-----------:|
| T | T | T |
| T | F | F |
| F | T | T |
| F | F | T |
</div>
:::info Example
If Earth is bigger than Jupiter, then $2+3=6$. This is a valid implication.
:::

::::

:::tip Auxiliaries to Implication
Suppose $P \rightarrow Q$ is an implication, then 
- $Q\rightarrow P$ is its **Converse**
- $\neg P\rightarrow \neg Q$ is its **Inverse**
- $\neg Q\rightarrow \neg P$ is its **Contrapositive**, and $P\rightarrow Q\equiv \neg Q\rightarrow \neg P$
:::

### Rules of Inference

We should not confuse iplication with inference, because inference is how we draw conclusion, and it needs follow from certain rules.

::::tip Modus Ponens
If $P$ istrue, and $P\rightarrow Q$ is true, then we infer that $Q$ is true, as: 
$$
\big(P\land (P\rightarrow Q) \big)\implies Q
$$
:::note Notation
Note that we use $\implies$ (\implies) only when we draw inference in this class, which is equivalent to "therefore"
:::
::::

:::tip Modus Tollens
If $\neg P$ is true, and $P\rightarrow Q$ is true, then weinfer that $\neg P$ is true, as:
$$
\big((\neg Q)\land (P\rightarrow Q)\big)\implies \neg P
$$
<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

From Modus Ponens:

$$
\big(\neg Q\land (\neg Q\rightarrow \neg P) \big)\implies \neg P
$$
According to $P\rightarrow Q\equiv \neg Q\rightarrow \neg P$:

$$
\big((\neg Q)\land (P\rightarrow Q)\big)\implies \neg P  \tag*{$\blacksquare$}
$$


</details>
:::
