### The Simplest Discrete Distribution

::::tip Bernoulli Trial

The simplest distribution is the Bernoulli Trial.

- Each has $2$ outcomes: "success" (1) and "failure" (0)

:::note
"success" and "failure" are just labels.
:::

:::info Example

Guessing Game: $2$ outcomes on a particular question

- Guess correctly: "Success"
- Guess incorrectly: "Failure"

$\mathbb{X}$ is the "score" on the question

- $\mathbb{X}=1$: guess correctly
- $\mathbb{X}=0$: guess incorrectly


<hr/>

:::

::::

:::tip Parametrization

What is the probability of observing either outcome? 

$$
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{X}=1|C)=\frac{1}{2}, \qquad \mathbb{P}(\mathbb{X}=0|C)=\frac{1}{2}
$$

However, in general, it depends on the specific context, which we need to **Parametrize**!

Suppose $p$ is the probability of observing "success" on the Bernoulli trial; by extension, the probability of "failure" is $1-p$.

:::

:::tip Distribution

For the distribution:

$$
\begin{align*}
    \mathbb{P}(\text{"success"}|C)&=\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{X}=1|C)=p\\
    \mathbb{P}(\text{"failure"}|C)&=\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{X}=0|C)=1-p
\end{align*}
$$

Moreover, if we determine $\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{X}=k|C)=M(k)$ for all observable outcomes $k$, then this probability becomes a function of $k$

For the Bernoulli trial:

$$
M(k)=\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{X}=k|C)=
\begin{cases}
1-p, & k=0\\
p, & k=1\\
0, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$

:::

### Named Distribution

:::tip 

If $\mathbb{X}$ has a Bernoulli distribution, we write $\mathbb{X}\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)$

:::

### Binomial Distribution

::::tip Binomial Distribution

The **Binomial Distribution** considers what would happen after $N$ independent, identical Bernoulli trials

Specifically, the Binomial Distribution counts the number of "successes" observed in $N$ trials

- What are the outcomes? $\underbrace{0,1,\dots, N-1,N}_{N+1\text{ outcomes}}$

- What are the parameters? 
    1. $p$, the probability of "success" on any one trial
    2. $N$, the number of trials

- Examine the probabilities of observing each outcome; recall the context specifies $\mathbb{X}\sim \operatorname{Binomial}(N,p)$

$$
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{X}=k|C)=M(k)
$$

We're looking for the probability of $k$ "successes" in $N$ trials.

$$
\underbrace{SS\cdots S}_{k \text{ successes}}\underbrace{FF\cdots F}_{N-k \text{ failures}}
$$

$$
\therefore \text{There are } \binom{N}{k}\text{ such outcomes}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
\mathbb{P}(S\cdots SF\cdots F)&=\mathbb{P}(S)\cdots \mathbb{P}(S)\mathbb{P}(F)\cdots \mathbb{P}(F)\\
                                &=[\mathbb{P}(\text{"success"}|C)]^{k}[\mathbb{P}(\text{"failure"}|C)]^{N-k}\\
                                &=p^{k}(1-p)^{N-k}
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\therefore \ \mathbb{P}(\mathbb{X}=k|\mathbb{X}\sim \operatorname{Binomial}(N,p))=M(k)=
\begin{cases}
\dbinom{N}{k}p^{k}(1-p)^{N-k}, & k=0,1,\dots, N\\[4pt]
0, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$

:::info Example

Back to our guessing game: 

    Score: # of correct guesses 

    $\mathbb{X}\sim \operatorname{Binomial}(N=10,p=\frac{1}{2})$

$$
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{X}=10|C)=M(10)=\binom{10}{10}\left(\frac{1}{2} \right)^{10}\left(1-\frac{1}{2} \right)^{10-10}=\frac{1}{1024} 
$$

:::
