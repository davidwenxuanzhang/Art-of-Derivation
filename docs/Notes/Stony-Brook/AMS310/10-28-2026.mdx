## Student T Distribution

Assume a normal population 

$$
\mathbb{X}_1, \mathbb{X}_2,\dots ,\mathbb{X}_n \text{ are independent and identically distributed, with }\qquad \mathbb{X}_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)
$$

::::tip Useful Properties

Suppose $\mu$ and $\sigma^2$ are known. The Central Limit Theorem becomes exact for all $N$.

:::info Example

Take the IQ test as an example: IQ is defined to have a normal distribution with mean $100$ and variance $225$. Suppose $30$ people have their IQ tested.

$$
\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}]=100,\qquad \operatorname{var}[\mathbb{X}]=\frac{\sigma^2}{N}=\frac{225}{30}=7.5
$$

$$
\mathbb{P}[\mathbb{X}\ge 120\mid \mathbb{X}\sim \mathcal{N}(100,7.5)]=1-\Phi\left(\frac{120-100}{\sqrt{7.5}}\right) \approx 0
$$

:::

::::

But what if the population variance $\sigma^2$ is missing? We can approximate the population variance $\sigma^2$ with our sample variance $\mathbb{S}^2$

:::tip Student T Distribution

Approximate the population variance $\sigma^2$ with our sample variance $\mathbb{S}^2$; however, this introduces more error, which results in more uncertainty, written as:

$$
\mathbb{X}\sim \mathcal{T}\left( \mu, \frac{\mathbb{S}^2}{N},N-1 \right)
$$
Above, we are taking the sample mean's error into account. By replacing the parameters:



- Parameters:
    - $\mu$: mean
    - $\gamma^2$: scaled factor 
    - $k$: degrees of freedom
    $$
    \mathbb{X}\sim \mathcal{T}(\mu,\gamma^2,k)
    $$

- Probability Density Function

$$
f[x\mid \mathbb{X}\sim \mathcal{T}(\mu,\gamma^2,k)]=\frac{\Gamma {\left(\frac{k+1}{2} \right)}}{\  \Gamma{\left(\frac{k}{2} \right)} \gamma\sqrt{\pi k}}\left[1+\frac{1}{k}\left(\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\right)^2 \right]
$$

For a large sample size:

$$
\mathcal{T}\left( \mu, \frac{\mathbb{S}^2}{N},N-1 \right)\underbrace{\longrightarrow}_{N\ge 30}\mathcal{N}\left(\mu,\frac{\mathbb{S}^2}{N}\right)
$$

:::

## Chi Square Distribution

Previously, we are focusing on sampling the mean; what will happen if we sample variance? In fact, it gives another distribution. 


:::tip Chi Square Distribution

Let $\mathbb{S}^2$ be our sample variance.
(office hour)
$$
\mathbb{S}^2=\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\mathbb{X}_i-\mathbb{X})^2
$$
$$
\frac{N-1}{\sigma^2}\mathbb{S}^2\sim \chi^2(N-1)\quad \text{or}\quad \mathbb{S}^2\sim \frac{\sigma^2}{N-1} \chi^2(N-1)
$$

This is called the **Chi Square Distribution**

- Parameter: 
    - $k$: degrees of freedom 

$$
\mathbb{X}\sim \chi^2 (k)
$$
- Probability Density Function
    
    $$
    f[x\mid \mathbb{X}\sim \chi^2 (k)]=\begin{cases}
    \dfrac{x^{\frac{k}{2}-1}e^{-\frac{x}{2}}}{2^{\frac{k}{2}}\Gamma{\left(\frac{k}{2} \right)}}, & x> 0\\[6pt]
    0, & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
    $$

For a large degree of freedom, this distribution (and hence its CDF) is well-approximated by a normal distribution:

$$
\chi^2(k)\underbrace{\longrightarrow}_{k\ge 30}\mathcal{N}(k,2k)
$$

- Expectation

$$
\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}]=k
$$

- Variance

$$
\operatorname{var}[\mathbb{X}]=2k
$$
:::
