The Simplest Discrete Distribution​

Bernoulli Trial

The simplest distribution is the Bernoulli Trial.

  • Each has 222 outcomes: "success" (1) and "failure" (0)
note

"success" and "failure" are just labels.

Example

Guessing Game: 222 outcomes on a particular question

  • Guess correctly: "Success"
  • Guess incorrectly: "Failure"

X\mathbb{X}X is the "score" on the question

  • X=1\mathbb{X}=1X=1: guess correctly
  • X=0\mathbb{X}=0X=0: guess incorrectly
Parametrization

What is the probability of observing either outcome?

P(X=1∣C)=12,P(X=0∣C)=12\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{X}=1|C)=\frac{1}{2}, \qquad \mathbb{P}(\mathbb{X}=0|C)=\frac{1}{2}P(X=1∣C)=21​,P(X=0∣C)=21​

However, in general, it depends on the specific context, which we need to Parametrize!

Suppose ppp is the probability of observing "success" on the Bernoulli trial; by extension, the probability of "failure" is 1−p1-p1−p.

Distribution

For the distribution:

P("success"∣C)=P(X=1∣C)=pP("failure"∣C)=P(X=0∣C)=1−p\begin{align*} \mathbb{P}(\text{"success"}|C)&=\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{X}=1|C)=p\\ \mathbb{P}(\text{"failure"}|C)&=\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{X}=0|C)=1-p \end{align*}P("success"∣C)P("failure"∣C)​=P(X=1∣C)=p=P(X=0∣C)=1−p​

Moreover, if we determine P(X=k∣C)=M(k)\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{X}=k|C)=M(k)P(X=k∣C)=M(k) for all observable outcomes kkk, then this probability becomes a function of kkk

For the Bernoulli trial:

M(k)=P(X=k∣C)={1−p,k=0p,k=10,otherwiseM(k)=\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{X}=k|C)= \begin{cases} 1-p, & k=0\\ p, & k=1\\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}M(k)=P(X=k∣C)=⎩⎨⎧​1−p,p,0,​k=0k=1otherwise​

Named Distribution​

tip

If X\mathbb{X}X has a Bernoulli distribution, we write X∼Bernoulli⁡(p)\mathbb{X}\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)X∼Bernoulli(p)

Binomial Distribution​

Binomial Distribution

The Binomial Distribution considers what would happen after NNN independent, identical Bernoulli trials

Specifically, the Binomial Distribution counts the number of "successes" observed in NNN trials

  • What are the outcomes? 0,1,…,N−1,N⏟N+1 outcomes\underbrace{0,1,\dots, N-1,N}_{N+1\text{ outcomes}}N+1 outcomes0,1,…,N−1,N​​

  • What are the parameters?

    1. ppp, the probability of "success" on any one trial
    2. NNN, the number of trials

  • Examine the probabilities of observing each outcome; recall the context specifies X∼Binomial⁡(N,p)\mathbb{X}\sim \operatorname{Binomial}(N,p)X∼Binomial(N,p)

P(X=k∣C)=M(k)\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{X}=k|C)=M(k)P(X=k∣C)=M(k)

We're looking for the probability of kkk "successes" in NNN trials.

SS⋯S⏟k successesFF⋯F⏟N−k failures\underbrace{SS\cdots S}_{k \text{ successes}}\underbrace{FF\cdots F}_{N-k \text{ failures}}k successesSS⋯S​​N−k failuresFF⋯F​​∴There are (Nk) such outcomes\therefore \text{There are } \binom{N}{k}\text{ such outcomes}∴There are (kN​) such outcomesP(S⋯SF⋯F)=P(S)⋯P(S)P(F)⋯P(F)=[P("success"∣C)]k[P("failure"∣C)]N−k=pk(1−p)N−k\begin{align*} \mathbb{P}(S\cdots SF\cdots F)&=\mathbb{P}(S)\cdots \mathbb{P}(S)\mathbb{P}(F)\cdots \mathbb{P}(F)\\ &=[\mathbb{P}(\text{"success"}|C)]^{k}[\mathbb{P}(\text{"failure"}|C)]^{N-k}\\ &=p^{k}(1-p)^{N-k} \end{align*}P(S⋯SF⋯F)​=P(S)⋯P(S)P(F)⋯P(F)=[P("success"∣C)]k[P("failure"∣C)]N−k=pk(1−p)N−k​∴ P(X=k∣X∼Binomial⁡(N,p))=M(k)={(Nk)pk(1−p)N−k,k=0,1,…,N0,otherwise\therefore \ \mathbb{P}(\mathbb{X}=k|\mathbb{X}\sim \operatorname{Binomial}(N,p))=M(k)= \begin{cases} \dbinom{N}{k}p^{k}(1-p)^{N-k}, & k=0,1,\dots, N\\[4pt] 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}∴ P(X=k∣X∼Binomial(N,p))=M(k)=⎩⎨⎧​(kN​)pk(1−p)N−k,0,​k=0,1,…,Notherwise​
