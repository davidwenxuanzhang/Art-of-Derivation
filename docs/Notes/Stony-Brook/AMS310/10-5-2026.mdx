## Continuous Distributions


Given a continuous distributions PMF:
$$
M[x\mid \mathbb{X}]=\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{X}=x\mid C)
$$
it is a measure of **precision** because a critical issue for continuous random variables, but for continuous random variable, we do not know the exact, precise measure of it. 

::::tip Probability Density Function
Therefore, **intervals** make more sense for continuous distributions, that is to say, consider:

$$
\mathbb{P}[a\le \mathbb{X}\le b\mid C]
$$

    and we can recast it in:

$$
\mathbb{P}[x\le \mathbb{X}\le x+\Delta x\mid C]
$$

    scale by the interval size:

$$
\frac{\mathbb{P}[x\le \mathbb{X}\le x+\Delta x\mid C]}{\Delta x}
$$

make $\Delta x$ small:

$$
f[x\mid \mathbb{X}]=\lim_{\Delta x\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\mathbb{P}[x\le \mathbb{X}\le x+\Delta x\mid C]}{\Delta x}
$$

and $f$ is the **probability density function**

:::note 

$f[x\mid \mathbb{X}]\mathrm{d}x=\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}$ is an infinitestimal probability

:::
