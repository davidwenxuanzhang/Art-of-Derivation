1. Observed test statistic $m$
2. False negative rate $\varepsilon_{-}$
3. If sampling, the sample size $N$

::::tip Experimental Design

Can we constrain the experimental parameters to obtain particular objectives? 

Consider the sample mean as the test statistic:

$$
\text{large sample},\ \sigma^2\text{ known}\implies \mathbb{X}\sim \mathcal{N}\left( \mu_0,\frac{\sigma^2}{N}\right)
$$

For a fixed false negative rate $\varepsilon_{-}$, the acceptance region is:

$$
\left[\mu_0-z_{\frac{\varepsilon_{-}}{2}}\frac{\sigma}{{\sqrt{N}}},\mu_0+z_{\frac{\varepsilon_{-}}{2}}\frac{\sigma}{{\sqrt{N}}} \right]
$$

If we want $\bar{x}$ to be detectable, that is: 

$$
\bar{x}<\mu_0-z_{\frac{\varepsilon_{-}}{2}}\frac{\sigma}{{\sqrt{N}}} \text{ or } \bar{x}>\mu_0+z_{\frac{\varepsilon_{-}}{2}}\frac{\sigma}{{\sqrt{N}}}
$$
then the sample size must satisfy:
$$
|\bar{x}-\mu_0|>z_{\frac{\varepsilon_{-}}{2}}\frac{\sigma}{{\sqrt{N}}}\implies \sqrt{N}> \frac{\sigma z_{\frac{\varepsilon_{-}}{2}}}{|\bar{x}-\mu_0|}\implies N>\left(\frac{\sigma z_{\frac{\varepsilon_{-}}{2}}}{|\bar{x}-\mu_0|}\right)^2
$$

:::warning Example

For IQ, the population mean is $\mu=100$ and the population standard deviation is $\sigma=15$. Suppose we want our test to detect $\bar{x}=105$; what is $N$? Suppose $\varepsilon_{-}=0.01$, then $z_{0.005}=2.57$:

$$
N> \left(\frac{15(2.57)}{5} \right)^2=59.4
$$

Therefore, the minimum sample size is $N=60$.

:::

::::

## Boundary of Acceptance and Rejection

Consider the question: when does $m$ cross from the acceptance region to the rejection region?

### Two-sided Scenario

$$
\theta=\theta_0,\qquad m<\text{median of }\mathbb{M}(\theta_0): m<Q[\frac{1}{2}\mid \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)]
$$
$$
m=Q[\frac{\varepsilon_{-}}{2}\mid \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)]\implies \frac{\varepsilon_{-}}{2}=F[m\mid \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)]
$$
$$
p=\varepsilon_{-}=2F[m\mid \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)]
$$

This value $p$ is called the $p$-value.

Similarly, if

$$
m>\text{median of }\mathbb{M}(\theta_0): m>Q[\frac{1}{2}\mid \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)]
$$
$$
m=Q[1-\frac{\varepsilon_{-}}{2}\mid \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)]\implies 1-\frac{\varepsilon_{-}}{2}=F[m\mid \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)]
$$
$$
\begin{align*}
p=\varepsilon_{-}&=2(1-F[m\mid \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)])\\
&=2S[m\mid \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)]
\end{align*}
$$

Combining these two:

$$
p=2\operatorname{min}\{F[m\mid \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)],S[m\mid \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)] \}
$$

### One-sided Scenario

- $\theta<\theta_0$
    $$
    m=Q[\varepsilon_{-}\mid \mathbb{M}{(\theta_0)}]
    $$
    $$
    p=\varepsilon_{-}=F[m\mid \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)]
    $$
- $\theta>\theta_0$
    $$
    m=Q[1-\varepsilon_{-}\mid \mathbb{M}{(\theta_0)}]
    $$
    $$
    p=\varepsilon_{-}=1- F[m\mid \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)]=S[m\mid \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)]
    $$

:::danger Remark

The $p$-value is the probability of observing something at least as extreme as our data if we repeat the experiment, assuming the hypothesis is true.

:::

## Statistical Significance Test

:::tip Statistical Significance Test

- Test statistic: $p$-value
    - High $p$: data consistent with the hypothesis
    - Low $p$: data inconsistent with the hypothesis

- Meta-hypothesis: the data are consistent with the original hypothesis.
    - Acceptance region: $(\alpha,1]$
    - Rejection region: $[0,\alpha)$

Typically, $\alpha=0.05$, and

- $p<0.05$: the result is statistically significant
- $p>0.05$: the result is not statistically significant



:::
