## Independence

:::tip Independence
If $\mathbb{X}$ and $\mathbb{Y}$ are independent, then $\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}\mathbb{Y}]=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{Y}]$ such that: 
$$
\operatorname{cov}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}\mathbb{Y}]-\mu_{\mathbb{X}}\mu_{\mathbb{Y}}=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{Y}]-\mu_{\mathbb{X}}\mu_{\mathbb{Y}}=0
$$
:::

## Linear Combination of Random Variables

::::tip Linear Combination of Random Variables

Consider the linear combination: 

$$
\mathbb{Y}=a_1\mathbb{X}_1+a_2\mathbb{X}_2+\dots+a_n\mathbb{X}_n
$$
Let

$$
\mu_1=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}_1],\quad \mu_2=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}_2],\dots, \quad \sigma^{2}_1=\operatorname{var}[\mathbb{X}_1],\quad \sigma^{2}_2=\operatorname{var}[\mathbb{X}_2],\dots
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{Y}]&=\mathbb{E}[a_1\mathbb{X}_1+a_2\mathbb{X}_2+\dots+a_n\mathbb{X}_n]\\
&=a_1\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}_1]+a_2\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}_2]+\dots+a_n\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}_n]\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}_i]\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i\mu_{i}
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{var}[\mathbb{Y}]&=\operatorname{var}[a_1\mathbb{X}_1+a_2\mathbb{X}_2+\dots+a_n\mathbb{X}_n]\\
&=a^2_1\operatorname{var}[\mathbb{X}_1]+a^2_2\operatorname{var}[\mathbb{X}_2]+\dots+a^2_n\operatorname{var}[\mathbb{X}_n] \qquad \text{(by independence)}\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i^2\sigma_i^2
\end{align*}
$$

:::info Example

Brand 1's cell phone battery holds a charge, on average, for 48 hours, with a variance of $8\text{ hours}^2$. Brand 2's phone battery lasts $60$ hours (on average), with a variance of $24\text{ hours}^2$.

$$
\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}_1]=48 \qquad \operatorname{var}[\mathbb{X}_1]=8 \qquad \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}_2]=60 \qquad \operatorname{var}[\mathbb{X}_2]=24
$$

1. Determine and interpret $\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}_1-\mathbb{X}_2]$

    $$
    \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}_1-\mathbb{X}_2]=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}_1]-\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}_2]=48-60=-12
    $$

    On average, brand 1's battery lasts $12$ hours less than brand 2's battery.

2. Assuming $\mathbb{X}_1$ and $\mathbb{X}_2$ are independent, determine $\operatorname{var}[\mathbb{X}_1-\mathbb{X}_2]$
    
    $$
    \begin{align*}
    \operatorname{var}[\mathbb{X}_1-\mathbb{X}_2]&=(1)^2\operatorname{var}[\mathbb{X}_1]+(-1)^2\operatorname{var}[\mathbb{X}_2]\\
    &=\operatorname{var}[\mathbb{X}_1]+\operatorname{var}[\mathbb{X}_2]\\
    &=8+24=32
    \end{align*}
    $$

:::

## Statistics

Consider how to make expectations about our data. The end goal is to get a model for the data. This combination of data and probability is statistics. 

### Data

:::tip Data

Each data point can be regarded as a random variable, which means every data point we collect has an expectation, variance, distribution, and so on, with uncertainty.

Critical dichotomy:

- A population is a vast (infinite or inexhaustible) collection of all potential observations. Colloquially, it is the distribution we **expect** for our data or is the "true" distribution for our data, etc.
- A sample consists of the observations we obtain (or might obtain) through experiments; that is, as a data set, a sample is finite.  

:::

### Properties

:::tip Properties

Properties of a population are called **Parameters**. Analogous properties of a sample are called **statistics**.

:::

Things to keep in mind: 

1. Samples always have what can be called **noise**, or **sampling error**.
2. The sample distribution and the population distribution need not be the same.
