## Independence

:::tip Independence
If $\mathbb{X}$ and $\mathbb{Y}$ are independent, then $\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}\mathbb{Y}]=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{Y}]$ such that: 
$$
\operatorname{cov}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}\mathbb{Y}]=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}\mathbb{Y}]-\mu_{\mathbb{X}}\mu_{\mathbb{Y}}=0
$$
:::

## Statistics

Consider how to make expectations for our data. The end goal is to get the model for data. This combination of data and probability is statistics. 

### Data

:::tip Data

Each data can be regarded as a random variable, which means every data we put has a expectation, varaince, distribution, and so on with uncertainty.

Critical dichotomy:

- A population is a vast (infinite or inexhaustible) correlation of all potential observations. Colloquially, it is the distribution we **expect** for our data or is the "true" distribution for our data, etc.
- A sample consists of the observation we obtain (or might obtain) through experiments; that is, as data set, a sample is finite.  

:::

### Properties

:::tip Properties

Properties of population are called **Parameters**. Analogous properties of samples are called **statistics**.

:::

Things to keep in mnd: 

1. Sample always have what can be called **noise**, or **sampling error**.
2. The sample distribution and the population distribution need not be the same.
