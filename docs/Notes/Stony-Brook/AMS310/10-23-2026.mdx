## Random Sample


:::tip Random Sample

Suupose we have $N$ data; that is, $N$ random variables $\mathbb{X}_1,\dots,\mathbb{X}_n$. Supppose the ${N}$ are independent and identically distributed (iid); that is, they all come form/sample the same population. This is called a **Random Sample**.

:::

### Mean of Random Sample

:::tip Mean of Random Sample

The mean of those random variables is itself a random variable 
$$
\mathbb{X}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\mathbb{X}_i}
$$ 
will be important. This is the **Sample Mean**.

$$
\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}]=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\mathbb{X}_i} \right]=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}_i]=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mu=\mu
$$

$\mu=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{X}_i]$ is the **Population Mean**

$$
\operatorname{var}[\mathbb{X}]=\operatorname{var}\left[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\mathbb{X}_1}\right]=\frac{1}{N^2}\operatorname{var}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\mathbb{X}_1}\right]=    \frac{1}{N^2}\operatorname{var}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\sigma^2}\right]=\frac{\sigma^2}{N}
$$
$\sigma^2=\operatorname{var}[\mathbb{X}_i]$ is the **Population Variance**

:::

### Variance of Random Sample


:::tip Variance of Random Sample


The sample variance is also a random variable:

$$
\mathbb{S}^2=\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\mathbb{X}_i-\mathbb{X})^2\qquad \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{S}^2]=\sigma^2
$$

:::

## Normal Distribution

:::tip Normal Distribution

The **Normal Distribution** is going to be useful

- Parameters: 
    - $\mu$: The mean
    - $\sigma^2$: The variance

    $$
    \mathbb{X}\sim\operatorname{normal}\left(\mu, \sigma^2\right)
    $$
    $\mathbb{X}\sim\operatorname{normal}(0, 1)$ is the standard normal distribution

- 
    $$
    f[x\mid \mathbb{X}]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi \sigma^2}}\operatorname{exp}\left[-\frac{(x-\mu )^2}{2\sigma^2} \right]
    $$

:::
