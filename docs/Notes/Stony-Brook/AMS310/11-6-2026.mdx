## Acceptance Interval

Setup: suppose $\mathbb{M}(\theta_0)$ is the model for the test statistic under a two-sided hypothesis $\theta=\theta_0$, and we acquire our data and observe a test statistic $m$.

:::danger Goal

Here, we want to determine an interval $[a,b]$ such that the probability of a false negative is $\varepsilon_{-}$, where the false negative rate $\varepsilon_{-}$ is chosen based on our goal.

:::

Recall from [Quantifying Error](./11-4-2026.mdx#quantifying-error) that the false negative rate is:

$$
\begin{align*}
    \varepsilon_{-}&=\mathbb{P}[\text{reject }\theta=\theta_0\mid \theta=\theta_0\text{ is true} ]\\
    &=1-\mathbb{P}[\text{accept } \theta=\theta_0\mid \theta=\theta_0\text{ is true} ]\\
    &=1-\mathbb{P}[a\le \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)\le b\mid C ]
\end{align*}
$$

We want to determine $a$ and $b$ as functions of $\varepsilon_{-}$, splitting $\varepsilon_{-}$ equally between the left and right tails.

:::danger Remark

Splitting $\varepsilon_{-}$ equally between the left and right tails is just the most accessible approach; there are other methods, but we will not discuss them here.

:::

$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{\varepsilon_{-}}{2}&=\mathbb{P}[\mathbb{M}(\theta_0)\le a\mid C]=F[a\mid \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)]\\
&\implies a=Q\left[\frac{\varepsilon_{-}}{2}\mid \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)\right]\\[6pt]
\frac{\varepsilon_{-}}{2}&=\mathbb{P}[\mathbb{M}(\theta_0)\ge b\mid C]=1-F[b\mid \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)]\\
&\implies b=Q\left[1-\frac{\varepsilon_{-}}{2}\mid \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)\right]
\end{align*}
$$

:::info Example

The test statistic is the sample mean and the random sample is sufficiently large to invoke the Central Limit Theorem.

- Hypothesis: $\mu=\mu_0$, assume $\sigma^2$ is known, and

$$
\mathbb{M}(\mu_0)\longrightarrow \mathbb{X}\sim \mathcal{N}\left(\mu_0,\frac{\sigma^2}{N}\right)
$$

- Fix $\varepsilon_{-}$; the acceptance region for our test is $[a(\varepsilon_{-}), b(\varepsilon_{-})]$

$$
\begin{align*}
    a&=Q\left[\frac{\varepsilon_{-}}{2}\mid \mathcal{N}\left(\mu_0,\frac{\sigma^2}{N}\right) \right]=\mu_0-z_{\frac{\varepsilon_{-}}{2}}\frac{\sigma }{\sqrt{N}}\\
    b&=Q\left[1-\frac{\varepsilon_{-}}{2}\mid \mathcal{N}\left(\mu_0,\frac{\sigma^2}{N}\right) \right]=\mu_0+z_{\frac{\varepsilon_{-}}{2}}\frac{\sigma }{\sqrt{N}}
\end{align*}
$$

This is also called a $z$ test. 

:::

:::tip Quantitative Example

IQ has a population mean $\mu=100$ and $\sigma^2=225$. If 30 people have their IQ measured, producing a sample mean of $\bar{x}=110$, are these 30 people representative of humanity?

- Hypothesis: $\mu=100$

    $$
    \mathbb{X}\sim \mathcal{N}\left(100,\frac{225}{30} \right)
    $$

- Tolerance of error: $\varepsilon_{-}=0.1$

- Acceptance region:

    $$
    \mu_0 \pm z_{\frac{\varepsilon_{-}}{2}}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{N}}=100\pm 1.65 \sqrt{\frac{225}{30}}=[95.48, 104.52]
    $$

    Or

    $$
    \begin{align*}
    a&=Q\left[\frac{\varepsilon_{-}}{2}\mid \mathcal{N}\left(\mu_0,\frac{\sigma^2}{N}\right) \right]= Q\left[\frac{0.1}{2}\mid \mathcal{N}\left(100,\frac{225}{30}\right) \right]=95.48\\
    b&=Q\left[1-\frac{\varepsilon_{-}}{2}\mid \mathcal{N}\left(\mu_0,\frac{\sigma^2}{N}\right) \right]=
    Q\left[1-\frac{0.1}{2}\mid \mathcal{N}\left(100,\frac{225}{30}\right) \right]=104.52
    \end{align*}
    $$

:::

## One-sided Test

One-sided hypothesis: $\theta<\theta_0 \quad\text{or}\quad \theta>\theta_0$

For $\theta<\theta_0$:

$$
\text{Acceptance}: (-\infty,a]\qquad \text{Rejection}: (a,\infty)
$$

Although $\theta=\theta_0$ is no longer the hypothesis being tested, we can still use the model $\mathbb{M}(\theta_0)$ when doing a one-sided test.

True positive for $\theta<\theta_0$:

$$
\begin{align*}
    &\mathbb{P}(\text{accept }\theta<\theta_0\mid \theta<\theta_0\text{ is true})\\
    =&\mathbb{P}(\text{reject }\theta=\theta_0 \text{ to the left}\mid \theta=\theta_0 \text{ is true})\\
    =&\varepsilon_{-}\\
    =&\mathbb{P}[\mathbb{M}(\theta_0)< a \mid C]\\
    =&F[a\mid \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)]\\
    \implies &a=Q[\varepsilon_{-}\mid \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)]
\end{align*}
$$

- For $\theta<\theta_0$: the acceptance region is $(-\infty , Q[\varepsilon_{-}\mid \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)]]$
- For $\theta>\theta_0$: the acceptance region is $[Q[1-\varepsilon_{-}\mid \mathbb{M}(\theta_0)],\infty)$
