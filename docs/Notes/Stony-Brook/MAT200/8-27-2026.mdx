## Terminologies

### Implication 

::::tip Implication

Implication is a statement of the form $P \implies Q$, meaning that "if $P$, then $Q$" or "$P$ **implies** $Q$", where $P$ is used as hypothesis and $Q$ follows as a conclusion.
<div style={{ width: 'fit-content', margin: '0 auto' }}>

| $P$ | $Q$ | $P \implies Q$ |
|:---:|:---:|:-----------:|
| T | T | T |
| T | F | F |
| F | T | T |
| F | F | T |
</div>
:::danger Remark
If $P\implies Q$, this does not give $P\implies Q$.
:::

:::info Example 

1. Let $P(x)>3$ and $Q(x)>0$, then $P\implies Q$  
    → **True**, because if $x>3$ then indeed $x>0$

2. For real $x$, $x^2=4 \implies x=2$   
    → **False**, because $x=-2$ also satisfies $x^2=4$, but $-2\ne 2$

3. Over the real $x$, $x=2\implies x^2=4$  
    → **True**
:::
:::note Alternative Expressions
There are many other alternative expressions for $P\implies Q$
- If $P$, then $Q$
- $P$ implies $Q$
- $Q$ if $P$
- $P$ only if $Q$
- $Q$ whenever $P$
- $P$ is sufficient for $Q$
- $Q$ is necessary for $P$
:::

::::

### Converse 

:::tip Converse

The **Converse** of $P\implies Q$ is  $Q\implies P$

:::


### Inverse 

:::tip Inverse

The **Inverse** of $P\implies Q$ is  $\neg P\implies \neg Q$

:::

### Contrapositive 

:::tip Contrapositive

The **Contrapositive** of $P\implies Q$ is  $\neg Q\implies \neg P$

:::

### Equivalent 

:::tip Equivalent 

$P$ is **Equivalent** to $Q$ if $P\iff Q$, meaning $P\implies Q$ and $Q\implies P$, read as "$P$ if and only if $Q$" (iff)

:::

## Lemma

:::tip Lemma 1
$$
P \implies Q \equiv \neg P \lor Q
$$
<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

<div style={{ width: 'fit-content', margin: '0 auto' }}>

| $P$ | $Q$ | $P \implies Q$ | $\neg P$ | $\neg P\lor Q$ |
|:---:|:---:|:-----------:|:---:|:---:|
| T | T | T | F | T |
| T | F | F | F | F |
| F | T | T | T | T |
| F | F | T | T | T |
</div>
<div style={{ textAlign: 'right' }}>
  $\blacksquare$
</div>
</details>
:::

:::tip Lemma 2

$$
P\implies Q \equiv \neg Q \implies\neg P
$$

<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

- $P\implies Q$ says that  if $P$ is true then $Q$ is true.
- Assume $\neg Q$ is true, $P$ cannot be true because that would imply $Q$ is true
- Hence $\neg P$ is true
<div style={{ textAlign: 'right' }}>
  $\blacksquare$
</div>
</details>
:::
