Terminologies​

Proposition​

Proposition

Proposition is a statement that is eith exclusively true or exclusively false, which means it cannot be both.

Example

  1. New York City is west of Stony Brook
    → A True Proposition

  2. New York City is east of Stony Brook
    → A False Proposition

  3. π=3\pi = 3π=3
    → A False Proposition

  4. nnn is a prime number
    → Not yet a Proposition, need domain

  5. n2−2n>0n^2-2n>0n2−2n>0
    → Not a Proposition, need domain

  6. n2≥0n^2 \ge 0n2≥0
    → Not a Proposition, need domain

Predicate​

Predicate

Predicate is a sentence containing one or more variables that become proposition once a value has assigned to the variables. These unassigned variables are called free variables.

Example: Distinguish Predicate and Proposition

  1. x+1=5x+1=5x+1=5
    → Predicate, where xxx is a free variable

  2. 2+1=52+1=52+1=5
    → A False Proposition

  3. For a real number xxx, x2≥0x^2\ge 0x2≥0
    → A True Proposition, because it has domain, and it is always true

Disjunction​

Disjunction

For two propositions PPP and QQQ, their disjunction, denoted by P∨QP \lor QP∨Q, read as PPP OR QQQ, is true if at least one of PPP and QQQ is true.

Conjunction

For two propositions PPP and QQQ, their conjunction, denoted by P∧QP \land QP∧Q, read as PPP AND QQQ, is true if both PPP and QQQ are true.

Negation

The negation of a statement PPP, denoted by ¬P\neg P¬P, read as NOT PPP, is the statement that reverses the truth value of PPP.

Tautology​

Tautology

A Tautology is a statement that is true for every assignment, denoted by ⊤P\top P⊤P

Example
  1. Let PPP be a statement, then P∨¬P≡⊤P\lor \neg P \equiv \topP∨¬P≡⊤, means P∨¬PP\lor \neg PP∨¬P is always true.
PPPQQQP∨¬QP \lor \neg QP∨¬Q (PPP OR (NOT QQQ))

Contradiction​

Contradiction

A Contradiction is a statement that is false for every assignment, denoted by ⊥P\bot P⊥P

Example
  1. Let PPP be a statement, then P∧¬P≡⊥P\land \neg P \equiv \botP∧¬P≡⊥, means P∧¬PP\land \neg PP∧¬P is always false.
