## Terminologies

### Proposition

::::tip Proposition

Proposition is a statement that is either exclusively true or exclusively false, which means it cannot be both.

:::info Example

1. New York City is west of Stony Brook  
    → A **True Proposition**

2. New York City is east of Stony Brook  
    → A **False Proposition**

3. $\pi = 3$  
    → A **False Proposition**

4. $n$ is a prime number   
    → **Not yet a Proposition**, need domain

5. $n^2-2n>0$   
    → **Not a Proposition**, need domain

6. $n^2 \ge 0$   
    → **Not a Proposition**, need domain

:::

::::

### Predicate

::::tip Predicate

Predicate is a sentence containing one or more variables that become proposition once a value has assigned to the variables. These unassigned variables are called free variables.

:::info Example: Distinguish Predicate and Proposition

1. $x+1=5$   
    → **Predicate**, where $x$ is a free variable

2. $2+1=5$    
    → A **False Proposition**

3. For a real number $x$, $x^2\ge 0$  
    → A **True Proposition**, because it has domain, and it is always true

:::

::::

### Disjunction

::::tip Disjunction

For two propositions $P$ and $Q$, their disjunction, denoted by $P \lor Q$, read as $P$ **OR** $Q$, is true if at least one of $P$ and $Q$ is true.
<div style={{ width: 'fit-content', margin: '0 auto' }}>

| $P$ | $Q$ | $P \lor Q$ ($P$ **OR** $Q$) |
|:---:|:---:|:-----------:|
| T | T | T |
| T | F | T |
| F | T | T |
| F | F | F |
</div>
:::info Example

The predicate $x\le 1$ can be written as the disjunction of two predicates, $P(x):x<1 $ and $Q(x):x=1$.

:::

::::

### Conjunction

::::tip Conjunction

For two propositions $P$ and $Q$, their conjunction, denoted by $P \land Q$, read as $P$ **AND** $Q$, is true if both $P$ and $Q$ are true.
<div style={{ width: 'fit-content', margin: '0 auto' }}>

| $P$ | $Q$ | $P \land Q$ ($P$ **AND** $Q$) |
|:---:|:---:|:-----------:|
| T | T | T |
| T | F | F |
| F | T | F |
| F | F | F |
</div>
:::info Example

The predicate $2 <x\le 4$ can be written as the conjunction of two predicates, $P(x):2<x $ and $Q(x):x\le 4$.

:::
::::

### Negation
::::tip Negation

The negation of a statement $P$, denoted by $\neg P$, read as **NOT** $P$, is the statement that reverses the truth value of $P$.
:::info Example
<div style={{ width: 'fit-content', margin: '0 auto' }}>

| Statement | Negation |
|:---------:|:--------:|
| $x>3$ | $x\le 3$ |
| $x\ge 5$ | $x<5$ |
| $x$ is even | $x$ is not even |
| Every student come to class | At least one student not come to class |
</div>
:::
::::

### Tautology

::::tip Tautology

A Tautology is a statement that is true for every assignment, denoted by $\top P$
:::info Example

1. Let $P$ be a statement, then $P\lor \neg P \equiv \top$, means $P\lor \neg P$ is always true.
<div style={{ width: 'fit-content', margin: '0 auto' }}>

| $P$ | $Q$ | $P \lor \neg Q$ ($P$ **OR** (NOT $Q$)) |
|:---:|:---:|:-----------:|
| T | F | T |
| F | T | T |

</div>
:::

::::

### Contradiction

::::tip Contradiction

A Contradiction is a statement that is false for every assignment, denoted by $\bot P$
:::info Example

1. Let $P$ be a statement, then $P\land \neg P \equiv \bot$, means $P\land \neg P$ is always false.
<div style={{ width: 'fit-content', margin: '0 auto' }}>

| $P$ | $Q$ | $P \land \neg Q$ ($P$ **OR** (NOT $Q$)) |
|:---:|:---:|:-----------:|
| T | F | F |
| F | T | F |
</div>

:::

::::

## De Morgan's Law {#DMLaw}

:::tip De Morgan's First Law
Let $P$ and $Q$ be two statements, then De Morgan's Second Law says:
$$
\neg(P\land Q)\equiv  \neg P\lor \neg Q
$$
<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>
<div style={{ width: 'fit-content', margin: '0 auto' }}>

| $P$ | $Q$ | $P \land Q$  | $\neg (P \land Q)$ | $\neg P$ | $\neg Q$ | $\neg P\lor \neg Q$ |
|:---:|:---:|:-----------:|:-----------:|:---:|:---:|:-----------:|
| T | T | T | F | F | F | F |
| T | F | F | T | F | T | T |
| F | T | F | T | T | F | T |
| F | F | F | T | T | T | T |
</div>
<div style={{ textAlign: 'right' }}>
  $\blacksquare$
</div>
</details>
:::

:::tip De Morgan's Second Law
Let $P$ and $Q$ be two statements, then De Morgan's Second Law says:
$$
\neg(P\lor Q) \equiv \neg P\land \neg Q
$$
<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>
<div style={{ width: 'fit-content', margin: '0 auto' }}>
| $P$ | $Q$ | $P \lor Q$  | $\neg (P \lor Q)$ | $\neg P$ | $\neg Q$ | $\neg P\land \neg Q$ |
|:---:|:---:|:-----------:|:-----------:|:---:|:---:|:-----------:|
| T | T | T | F | F | F | F |
| T | F | T | F | F | T | F |
| F | T | T | F | T | F | F |
| F | F | F | T | T | T | T |
</div>
<div style={{ textAlign: 'right' }}>
  $\blacksquare$
</div>
</details>
:::
