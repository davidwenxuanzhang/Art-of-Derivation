**Geometrically:**

Vector is an **arrow** that has both magnitude and direction: 

<svg viewBox="0 0 140 90" width="100%" style={{ maxWidth: '160px', height: 'auto', display: 'block', margin: '0.6rem auto 0.2rem' }} fill="none" stroke="currentColor" strokeWidth="2" strokeLinecap="round" strokeLinejoin="round" role="img" aria-label="An arrow labeled v representing a vector"><line x1="15" y1="75" x2="110" y2="20"/><polyline points="102,30 110,20 97,22"/><text x="54" y="38" fontSize="16" fontStyle="italic" fontWeight="700" fill="currentColor" stroke="none">v</text></svg>

Two arrows define the same vector if one can be translated into the other. Equivalently, they define the same vector if they have the same magnitude and direction.

  </div>

  <div
    style={{
      paddingLeft: "2rem",
    }}
  >

**Analytically**

A vector in $\mathbb{R}^2$ (plane) or in $\mathbb{R}^3$ (space) is an ordered pair of real numbers $(x,y)$ or an ordered triple of real numbers $(x,y,z)$, as:

$$
\mathbf{v}=(x,y)\qquad \text{or} \qquad \mathbf{v}=(x,y,z)
$$

The numbers $x,y,z$ are the **components** of $\mathbf{v}$. More generally, a vector in $\mathbb{R}^n$ is an ordered $n$-tuple $(x_1,\dots,x_n)$.

Two vectors are equal exactly when their components match.

  </div>
</div>

### Properties

:::info Properties
$$
\begin{align*}
    1.\  &\mathbf{v}_1+\mathbf{v}_2=\mathbf{v}_2+\mathbf{v}_2 &\qquad \text{(Communicative)}\\
    2.\  &(\mathbf{v}_1+\mathbf{v}_2)+\mathbf{v}_3=\mathbf{v}_1+(\mathbf{v}_2+\mathbf{v}_3) &\qquad \text{(Additivity)}\\
    3.\  &c(\mathbf{v}_1+\mathbf{v}_2)=c\mathbf{v}_1+c\mathbf{v}_2 & \qquad \text{(Distributivity)}\\
\end{align*}
$$
:::

**Geometrically**

Given two vectors $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$, shown as below:

<svg viewBox="0 0 160 104" width="100%" style={{ maxWidth: '210px', height: 'auto', display: 'block', margin: '0.7rem auto 0.3rem' }} fill="none" stroke="currentColor" strokeWidth="2" strokeLinecap="round" strokeLinejoin="round" role="img" aria-label="Two vectors a and b from a common origin with the angle theta between them"><circle cx="20" cy="90" r="2" fill="currentColor" stroke="none"/><line x1="20" y1="90" x2="140" y2="60"/><polyline points="132,66 140,60 130,58"/><line x1="20" y1="90" x2="90" y2="25"/><polyline points="86,34 90,25 81,28"/><path d="M49.1,82.7 A30,30 0 0 0 42,69.6" strokeWidth="1.4" strokeOpacity="0.7"/><text x="142" y="70" fontSize="13" fontStyle="italic" fontWeight="700" fill="currentColor" stroke="none">a</text><text x="86" y="19" fontSize="13" fontStyle="italic" fontWeight="700" fill="currentColor" stroke="none">b</text><text x="55" y="73" fontSize="12" fontStyle="italic" fill="currentColor" stroke="none">θ</text></svg>

their **Dot Product** is:

$$
\mathbf{a}\cdot \mathbf{b}=\|\mathbf{a}\|\|\mathbf{b}\|\cos{\theta}
$$

  </div>

  <div
    style={{
      paddingLeft: "2rem",
    }}
  >

**Analytically**

Given two vectors:
$$
\mathbf{v}=(v_1,v_2,v_3) \qquad \text{and}\qquad \mathbf{w}=(w_1,w_2,w_3)
$$
Their **Dot Product** is:

$$
\mathbf{v}\cdot \mathbf{w}=v_1w_1+v_2w_2+v_3w_3
$$
