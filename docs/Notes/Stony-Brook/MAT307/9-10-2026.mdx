### Binormal Vector

::::tip Binormal Vector

For the curve in $\mathbb{R}^3$, the **Binormal Vector** is:

$$
\mathbf{B}(s)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{\coloneqq} \mathbf{T}(s)\times \mathbf{N}(s)
$$

:::info Observation

Because $\mathbf{T}$ is the tangent unit vector, $\mathbf{N}$ is the normal unit vector, and $\mathbf{T}\perp \mathbf{N}$:

$$
\|\mathbf{B}\|=\mathbf{T}(s)\times \mathbf{N}(s)=\|\mathbf{T}(s)\|\|\mathbf{N}(s)\|\sin{90^{\circ}}=1
$$

:::

:::info Propositions

1. 

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{B}(s)\perp \mathbf{B}(s)
$$

<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

$$
\begin{align*}
    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\big(\underbrace{\mathbf{B}(s) \cdot \mathbf{B}(s) }_{\mathclap{=1\text{ under observation}}}\big)&=0\\
    \implies 2\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{B}(s)\cdot \mathbf{B}(s) \right)&=0\\
    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{B}(s)\cdot \mathbf{B}(s)&=0 \tag*{$\blacksquare$}
\end{align*}
$$

</details>

2. 

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{B}(s)\perp \mathbf{T}(s)
$$

<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

$$
\begin{align*}
    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{B}(s)&=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\big(\mathbf{T}(s)\times \mathbf{N}(s) \big)\\
                                               &=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{T}(s)\times \mathbf{N}(s) +\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{N}(s) \times \mathbf{T}(s)\\
                                               &=\kappa(s)\underbrace{\mathbf{N}(s)\times \mathbf{N}(s)}_{0}+\mathbf{T}(s)\times \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{N}(s)\\
                                               &=\mathbf{T}(s)\times \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{N}(s)\\
\end{align*}
$$
As $\mathbf{T}(s)\times \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{N}(s)$ is orthogonal to $\mathbf{T}(s)$, therefore:
$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{B}(s)\perp \mathbf{T}(s) \tag*{$\blacksquare$}
$$

</details>

:::

### Torsion

::::tip Torsion

A corollary from the binormal vector is:

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{B}(s)=-\tau \mathbf{N}(s)
$$

where $\tau$ is a scalar function called the **Torsion** of the curve.

:::note Geometric Intuition

Torsion tells us how much the curve is "twisting".

:::

:::warning Exercise

Prove the following criterion: A curve $\gamma (s)$ is a planar if and only if the torsion $\tau(s)=0$ for any $s$.

<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

- Only If Direction: 

Given a curve $\gamma (s)$ is planar, we know it has a normal vector $\mathbf{k}$, then:

$$
\gamma(s)\cdot \mathbf{k}=\text{Constant}
$$

Take the derivative on both side:

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\big(\gamma(s)\cdot \mathbf{k}\big)=0\implies \mathbf{T}(s)\cdot \mathbf{k}=0 \implies \mathbf{k} \perp \mathbf{T}(s)
$$

Take the derivative on both side again:

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\big(\mathbf{T}(s)\cdot \mathbf{k}\big)=\kappa (s)\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{k}=0\implies \mathbf{k}\perp \mathbf{N}
$$

Since $\mathbf{B}(s)=\mathbf{T}(s) \times \mathbf{N}(s) $, meaning that $\mathbf{B}(s) \perp \mathbf{T}(s)$ and $\mathbf{B}(s) \perp \mathbf{N}(s)$. Therefore, the normal vector $\mathbf{k}$ is proportional to $\mathbf{B}(s)$ with same direction, which implies:

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{B}(s)=0 \implies \tau(s)\mathbf{N}(s)=0 \implies  \tau(s)=0
$$

<hr />

- If Direction: 

Assume $\tau(s)=0$, then :

$$
    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{B}(s)=-\tau(s)\mathbf{N}(s)=0 \implies \mathbf{B}(s) \text{ is a constant}
$$

Then, notice the derivative:

$$
\begin{align*}
    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\big(\gamma{(s)}\cdot \mathbf{B}(s) \big)^{\prime}&=\gamma^{\prime}(s)\cdot \mathbf{B}(s)+\gamma(s)\cdot \underbrace{\mathbf{B}^{\prime}(s)}_{0} &\qquad \text{(Dot Product Rule)}\\
            &=\mathbf{T}(s)\cdot \mathbf{B}(s)\\
            &=0 &\qquad (\mathbf{T}(s)\perp \mathbf{B}(s))
\end{align*}
$$
Hence, $\gamma(s)\cdot \mathbf{B}(s)$ is a constant, which suggests that $\gamma(s)$ is a plane with normal vector $\mathbf{B}(s)$ according to the general form of a [plane](docs/Notes/Stony-Brook/MAT307/8-27-2026.mdx#Planes).

<hr />
Therefore, 

$$
\gamma(s) \text{ is a planar } \iff \tau(s)=0 \text{ for all } s   \tag*{$\blacksquare$}
$$

</details>

:::

## Frenet–Serret Formula

We start with a leading question:

::::warning Question 

Compute  $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s) $ in terms of $\kappa(s), \tau(s), \mathbf{T}(s), \text{and } \mathbf{B}(s) $

:::info Solution

Because $\mathbf{T}, \mathbf{N}, \mathbf{B}$ construct a orthogonal basis at any point, so any vectors in $\mathbb{R}^3$, including $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)$, could be written as:

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)= a(s) \mathbf{T}(s)+b(s)\mathbf{N}(s)+c(s)\mathbf{B}(s)
$$

Also, because $\mathbf{T}, \mathbf{N}, \mathbf{B}$ are unit vectors and orthogonal to each other:

$$
\begin{align*}
    a(s)&=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{T}(s)\\
    b(s)&=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{N}(s)\\
    c(s)&=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{B}(s)
\end{align*}
$$

As $\mathbf{N}(s)$ is a unit vector: 

$$
\begin{align*}
    \mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{N}(s)&=1\\
    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\big(\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{N}(s) \big)&=0\\
    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{N}(s)&=0 \implies b(s)=0
\end{align*}
$$
Therefore: 

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)= a(s) \mathbf{T}(s)+c(s)\mathbf{B}(s)
$$
<hr />


From $\mathbf{T}(s)\cdot \mathbf{N}(s)=0$ and :

$$
\begin{align*}
    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\big( \mathbf{T}(s)\cdot \mathbf{N}(s) \big)&=0\\
                    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{T}(s)\cdot \mathbf{N}(s)+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{T}(s)&=0 &\qquad \text{(Dot Product Rule)}\\
                     a(s)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{T}(s)&=-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{T}(s)\cdot \mathbf{N}(s)\\
\end{align*}
$$

Given the fact that $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{T}(s)= - \kappa(s) \mathbf{N}(s)$

$$
\begin{align*}
   a(s)= \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{T}(s)&=-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{T}(s)\cdot \mathbf{N}(s)\\
    &=- \kappa (s)\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot  \mathbf{N}(s)\\
    &=-\kappa(s) \big(\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot {\mathbf{N}(s)} \big)\\
    &=-\kappa(s)
\end{align*}
$$

<hr />

From $\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{B}(s)=0$

$$
\begin{align*}
    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\big( \mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{B}(s) \big)&=0\\
                    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{B}(s)+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{B}(s)\cdot \mathbf{N}(s)&=0 &\qquad \text{(Dot Product Rule)}\\
                     c(s)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{B}(s)&=-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{B}(s)\cdot \mathbf{N}(s)\\
\end{align*}
$$

Given the fact that $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{B}(s)= - \tau (s)\mathbf{N}(s)$

$$
\begin{align*}
   c(s)= \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{B}(s)&=-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{B}(s)\cdot \mathbf{N}(s)\\
    &=- \tau (s)\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot  \mathbf{N}(s)\\
    &=-\tau (s)\big(\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot {\mathbf{N}(s)} \big)\\
    &=-\tau(s)
\end{align*}
$$
<hr />

Hence: 

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)= \kappa (s)\mathbf{T}(s)+\tau(s)\mathbf{B}(s)
$$
:::

This leads to the Frenet–Serret Formula
::::

::::tip Frenet–Serret Formula

Given the unit [tangent](docs/Notes/Stony-Brook/MAT307/9-8-2026.mdx#unit-tangent-vector), [normal](docs/Notes/Stony-Brook/MAT307/9-8-2026.mdx#unit-normal-vector), and [binormal](#binormal-vector) vector $\mathbf{T}(s),\mathbf{N}(s),\mathbf{B}(s)$ of a curve, we have:

$$
\begin{align*}
    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{T}(s)&= \kappa (s)\mathbf{N}(s)\\
    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)&= \kappa (s)\mathbf{T}(s)+\tau(s)\mathbf{B}(s)\\
    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{B}(s)&= \tau (s)\mathbf{N}(s)\\
\end{align*}
$$
where $\kappa(s)$ is the curvature [curvature](docs/Notes/Stony-Brook/MAT307/9-8-2026.mdx#curvature), and $\tau (s)$ is the [torsion](#torsion).
:::note 
These equations determine the shape of the curve up to an isometry.
:::
