### Binormal Vector

::::tip Binormal Vector

For the curve in $\mathbb{R}^3$, the **Binormal Vector** is:

$$
\mathbf{B}(s)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{\coloneqq} \mathbf{T}(s)\times \mathbf{N}(s)
$$


<div className="deriv2col">

  <div>

(for claude code: add a graph of a curve with its unit tangent vector and the normal vector and also a binormal vector shows orthogonal to both other curves)

  </div>

  <div>

$\mathbf{B}$ give us the normal direction of the tangent plane to the curve.   
  
  </div>

</div>

:::info Observation

Because $\mathbf{T}$ is the tangent unit vector, $\mathbf{N}$ is the normal unit vector, and $\mathbf{T}\perp \mathbf{N}$:

$$
\|\mathbf{B}\|=\mathbf{T}(s)\times \mathbf{N}(s)=\|\mathbf{T}(s)\|\|\mathbf{N}(s)\|\sin{90^{\circ}}=1
$$

:::

:::info Propositions

1. 

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{B}(s)\perp \mathbf{B}(s)
$$

<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

$$
\begin{align*}
    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\big(\underbrace{\mathbf{B}(s) \cdot \mathbf{B}(s) }_{\mathclap{=1\text{ under observation}}}\big)&=0\\
    \implies 2\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{B}(s)\cdot \mathbf{B}(s) \right)&=0\\
    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{B}(s)\cdot \mathbf{B}(s)&=0 \tag*{$\blacksquare$}
\end{align*}
$$

</details>

2. 

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{B}(s)\perp \mathbf{T}(s)
$$

<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

$$
\begin{align*}
    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{B}(s)&=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\big(\mathbf{T}(s)\times \mathbf{N}(s) \big)\\
                                               &=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{T}(s)\times \mathbf{N}(s) +\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{N}(s) \times \mathbf{T}(s)\\
                                               &=\kappa(s)\underbrace{\mathbf{N}(s)\times \mathbf{N}(s)}_{0}+\mathbf{T}(s)\times \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{N}(s)\\
                                               &=\mathbf{T}(s)\times \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{N}(s)\\
\end{align*}
$$
As $\mathbf{T}(s)\times \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{N}(s)$ is orthogonal to $\mathbf{T}(s)$, therefore:
$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{B}(s)\perp \mathbf{T}(s) \tag*{$\blacksquare$}
$$

</details>

:::

### Torsion

::::tip Torsion

A corollary from the binormal vector is:

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{B}(s)=-\tau \mathbf{N}(s)
$$

where $\tau$ is a scalar function called the **Torsion** of the curve.

:::note Geometric Intuition

Torsion tells us how much the curve is "twisting".

:::

:::warning Exercise

Prove the following criterion: A curve $\gamma (s)$ is a planar if and only if the torsion $\tau(s)=0$ for any $s$.

<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

If the curve is planar, then the plane has a normal direction $\mathbf{B}(s)$, which implies that $\mathbf{B}(s)$ is constant, which implies:

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{B}(s)=o \implies \tau(s)\mathbf{N}(s)=0 \implies  \tau(s)=0
$$

</details>

:::
