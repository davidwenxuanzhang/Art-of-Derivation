## Solving Linear Systems

::::tip Goal

Recall from previous lecture that our **Goal** is to find a systematic way to solve a system of linear equations:

$$
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}
$$

that is to say, we want to find the vector $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^n$ such that:

$$
\begin{cases}
a_{11}x_1+\cdots+a_{1n}x_n=b_1,\\
\qquad \vdots\\
a_{m1}x_1+\cdots+a_{mn}x_n=b_m.
\end{cases}
$$


:::info Example

$$
\begin{cases}
x_1 + 3x_2 + x_3 = 1\\
2x_1 + 4x_2 + 7x_3 = 2\\
4x_1 + 10x_2 + 9x_3 = 4
\end{cases}
\implies
A=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&3&1\\
2&4&7\\
4&10&9
\end{bmatrix} \quad
\mathbf{b}=
\begin{bmatrix}
1\\
2\\
4
\end{bmatrix}
$$

:::

::::

:::note Procedure

Our **General Idea** is to transform the system of equations into a simpler system of equations

:::

### Elementary Row Operations

For the example: 

:::info Example

We will use **Gaussian Elimination** to simplify the following equations step by step:

$$
\begin{cases}
x_1 + 3x_2 + x_3 = 1    &\qquad {(R_1)}\\
2x_1 + 4x_2 + 7x_3 = 2  &\qquad {(R_2)}\\
4x_1 + 10x_2 + 9x_3 = 4 &\qquad {(R_3)}
\end{cases}
$$

1. We want to keep $x_1$ in only one equation.


$$
\begin{cases}
x_1+3x_2+x_3=1\\
-2x_2+5x_3=0 &\qquad {(R_2-2R_1)}\\
-2x_2+5x_3=0 &\qquad {(R_3-4R_1)}
\end{cases}
$$

2. We want to keep $x_2$ in only two equations (with coefficients)

$$
\begin{cases}
x_1+3x_2+x_3=1 \\
x_2-\frac{5}{2}x_3=0 & \qquad {(-\frac{1}{2}R_2)}\\
0=0 &\qquad {(R_3-R_2)} 
\end{cases}
$$

We are at a good position to write down the solutions: 

$$
x_2-\frac{5}{2}x_3=0 \implies x_2=\frac{5}{2}x_3
$$
$$
x_1+3x_2+x_3=1\implies x_1=1-3x_2-x_3=1-\frac{15}{2}x_3-x_3=1-\frac{17}{2}x_3
$$

Now, if I use $x_3=t$ as the parameter for the solutions, I may obtain the solution set that has the following form:

$$
S=\{ \mathbf{x}\in \mathbb{R}^3 \text{ s.t. } \mathbf{x}=\left(1+\frac{17}{2}t,\frac{5}{2}t,t \right)  \text{ for some }t\in \mathbb{R}\}
$$

And we can parametrize in the equation of a line, as:

$$
\mathbf{x}=t\left(-\frac{17}{2}, \frac{5}{2}, 1 \right)+(1,0,0)
$$

:::

:::note Observation

I can do all these operations at the level of $(A\mid \mathbf{b})$ as a matrix; that is, I do not need to write down all those $x_i$s every time.

:::

:::tip Operations

Below are some elementary operations that do not change the solution set of the matrix $(A\mid \mathbf{b})$.

- We can exchange two rows.
- We can take a row and add a multiple of another row.
- We can take a row and multiply it by a non-zero coefficient.

:::

### Echelon Form

With those operations, any matrix can be reduced to the following form.

::::tip Echelon Form

The **Echelon Form** of a matrix looks like the following:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
0 & \cdots & 0 & \boxed{1} & * & \cdots & * & * & \cdots & * \\
0 & \cdots & 0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & \boxed{1} & * & \cdots & * \\
0 & \cdots & 0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & 0 & \cdots & \boxed{1} \\
0 & \cdots & 0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0
\end{bmatrix}
\quad
\begin{aligned}
\boxed{1}&: \text{pivot}\\
*&: \text{any number}
\end{aligned}
$$

:::info Properties

1. It has a staircase shape.
2. Below the stairs, we have only zeros.
3. Moving down the rows, the pivot moves to the right.
4. Columns containing pivots are called **pivot columns**.

:::
::::

### Basis

::::tip Basis

A set of vectors $\{\mathbf{v}_1,\dots , \mathbf{v}_k \} \subset V$ is called a **basis** for $V$ if it is both [Linearly Independent](#linear-independence) and a [System of Generators](#system-of-generators)

:::info Example

In the above example, $\{\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2\}$ is a **basis** (check that they are linearly independent)

:::

::::

:::tip Fact on Cardinality

Given a vector space $V$, any two bases $B_1$ and $B_2$ have the same **Cardinality** (the number of elements)

:::

### Dimension

::::tip Dimension

We can define the **Dimension** of a vector space $V$ as the cardinality of a basis $B$ for $V$, denoted as:

$$
\operatorname{dim}{(V)}
$$

:::warning Question
What is the dimension of $\mathbb{R}^n$?

<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

It is $n$! (this is an exclamation mark, not factorial)

Let $B=\{\mathbf{e}_1,\dots,\mathbf{e}_n \}$ be a basis:

$$
B=
\left\{
\begin{bmatrix}
1\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0
\end{bmatrix},
\ldots,
\begin{bmatrix}
0\\
\vdots\\
0\\
1
\end{bmatrix}
\right\}
$$

Indeed that:

- Given $\mathbf{x}=(x_1,\dots ,x_n)$

$$
\implies \mathbf{x}=x_1\mathbf{e}_1+\dots+x_n\mathbf{e}_n \implies B \text{ is a system of generators}
$$


- Suppose that $c_1\mathbf{e}_1+\dots + c_n\mathbf{e}_n=0$

$$
\implies (c_1,c_2,\dots ,c_n)=(0,0,\dots,0) \implies c_i=0 \text{ for any }i \implies B \text{ is linearly independent} \tag*{$\blacksquare$}
$$

</details>

## Image and Rank

### Matrix Associated to a Linear Map

:::tip Matrix Associated to a Linear Map

Recall that the linear map $\varphi:\mathbb{R}^n\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^m$ corresponds to its associated matrix:

$$
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^n\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^m
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
A=
\begin{bmatrix}
a_{11} & \cdots & a_{1n}\\
\vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\
a_{m1} & \cdots & a_{mn}
\end{bmatrix}
=
\big(
\varphi(\mathbf{e}_1),
\dots,
\varphi(\mathbf{e}_n)
\big)
$$

:::

### Image

:::tip Image of Linear Map

The **Image** of a linear map is a vector space, and the column vectors of its associated matrix form a system of generators for the image, denoted as:

$$
\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)=\operatorname{span}\{\text{columns of }A\}
$$

<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

Take $\mathbf{v}_1,\mathbf{v}_2\in \operatorname{Im}(\varphi)$ 

- $\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)$ is a vector space:
    $$
    \implies \mathbf{v}_1=\varphi(\mathbf{x}_1)\qquad \mathbf{v}_2=\varphi(\mathbf{x}_2)
    $$
    $$
    \implies \mathbf{v}_1+\mathbf{v}_2=\varphi(\mathbf{x}_1)+\varphi(\mathbf{x}_2)=\varphi(\mathbf{x}_1+\mathbf{x}_2)\in \operatorname{Im}(\varphi)
    $$
    Same for $c\mathbf{v}$:
    $$
    c\mathbf{v}_1=c\varphi(\mathbf{x}_1)=\varphi(c\mathbf{x}_1)\in \operatorname{Im}(\varphi)
    $$

- $\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)=\operatorname{span}\{\text{columns of }A\}$
    $$
    \mathbf{x}=x_1\mathbf{e}_1+\dots+x_n\mathbf{e}_n\implies \varphi(\mathbf{x})=x_1\varphi(\mathbf{e}_1)+\dots+x_n\varphi(\mathbf{e}_n)
    $$
    so any vector in the image can be written as a linear combination of the columns of $A$.
Therefore: 
$$
\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)=\operatorname{span}\{\text{columns of }A\} \tag*{$\blacksquare$}
$$


</details>

:::


### Rank

::::tip Rank

The **Dimension** of an image $\operatorname{dim}\big(\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)\big)$ is equal to the maximum number of linearly independent columns. This is also called the **Rank** of $\varphi$, denoted as $\operatorname{rank}(\varphi)$, or the **Rank** of $A$, denoted as $\operatorname{rank}(A)$

::::

### Final Remark

:::tip Final Remark

A linear system $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$ (equivalently, $\varphi(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{b}$) has a solution if and only if $\mathbf{b}\in \operatorname{Im}(\varphi)$.

Since $\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)=\operatorname{span}\{\text{columns of }A\}$, this means $\mathbf{b}$ must be a linear combination of the columns of $A$ — that is, adding $\mathbf{b}$ as a new column to $A$ should not increase the number of linearly independent columns. Hence:

$$
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b} \text{ has a solution} \iff \operatorname{rank}([A\mid\mathbf{b}])=\operatorname{rank}(A)
$$

If $\operatorname{rank}([A\mid\mathbf{b}])>\operatorname{rank}(A)$, then $\mathbf{b}$ is not in the span of the columns of $A$, so the system has no solution.

:::
