## Recap

### Linear Maps and Associated Matrix

:::tip Linear Map
Recall that a linear map $\varphi : V\rightarrow W$ is a function satisfying:

$$
\begin{align*}
    \varphi(\mathbf{v}_1+\mathbf{v}_2)&=\varphi(\mathbf{v}_1)+\varphi(\mathbf{v}_2)\\
    \varphi(c\mathbf{v})&=c\varphi(\mathbf{v})
\end{align*}
$$
:::

:::tip Associated Matrix
If $V=\mathbb{R}^n$ and $W=\mathbb{R}^m$, then the transformation of $\varphi$ has an associated matrix $A$:

$$
A=\big(\varphi(\mathbf{e}_1)\mid\varphi(\mathbf{e}_2)\mid \dots \mid \varphi(\mathbf{e}_n ) \big)
$$
where $\mathbf{e}_i$ is the [standard basis](docs\Notes\Stony-Brook\MAT307\9-15-2026.mdx#Standard-Basis), and $\varphi(\mathbf{e}_i)$ is column vector.

Therefore, the linear map could expressed as:

$$
\begin{align*}
    \varphi(\mathbf{x}_1,\dots,\mathbf{x}_n)&=A\mathbf{x}\\
                                            &=\begin{bmatrix}
a_{11}\mathbf{x}_1+a_{12}\mathbf{x}_2+\cdots+a_{1n}\mathbf{x}_n\\
a_{21}\mathbf{x}_1+a_{22}\mathbf{x}_2+\cdots+a_{2n}\mathbf{x}_n\\
\vdots\\
a_{m1}\mathbf{x}_1+a_{m2}\mathbf{x}_2+\cdots+a_{mn}\mathbf{x}_n
\end{bmatrix}
\end{align*}
$$

:::

## Composition of Linear Maps and Matrix Multiplication

::::tip Propositions

- Consider linear maps $\varphi:V\rightarrow W$ and $\psi:W\rightarrow Z$, then:


    $$
    \psi \circ \varphi : V\rightarrow Z
    $$
    is also a linear map

<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

Recall that for a valid [vector space](docs\Notes\Stony-Brook\MAT307\9-15-2026.mdx#Vector-Space), it has to satisfy both:

- Addition

$$
\psi\big(\varphi(\mathbf{v}_1+\mathbf{v}_2) \big)= \psi(\varphi(\mathbf{v}_1)+\varphi(\mathbf{v}_2))= \psi (\varphi(\mathbf{v}_1))+\psi (\varphi(\mathbf{v}_2))
$$

- Scalar Multiplication

$$
\psi\big(\varphi(c\mathbf{v}) \big)= \psi\big(c\varphi(\mathbf{v}) \big)=c\psi\big(\varphi(\mathbf{v}) \big)
$$

</details>

- Let $V=\mathbb{R}^n$, $W=\mathbb{R}^m$, and $Z=\mathbb{R}^{\ell}$. If $A=[\mathbf{c}_1\mid \dots \mid \mathbf{c}_n]$ is the matrix associated to $\varphi$ and $B=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{r}_1\\
\mathbf{r}_2\\
\vdots\\
\mathbf{r}_\ell
\end{bmatrix}$ is the matrix associated to $\psi$, then the matrix associated with teh composition of $\psi \circ \varphi$ is the matrix:

$$
BA= \begin{pmatrix}
\mathbf{r}_1\cdot \mathbf{c}_1 & \mathbf{r}_1\cdot \mathbf{c}_2 & \cdots & \mathbf{r}_1\cdot \mathbf{c}_n\\
\mathbf{r}_2\cdot \mathbf{c}_1 & \mathbf{r}_2\cdot \mathbf{c}_2 & \cdots & \mathbf{r}_2\cdot \mathbf{c}_n\\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\
\mathbf{r}_\ell\cdot \mathbf{c}_1 & \mathbf{r}_\ell\cdot \mathbf{c}_2 & \cdots & \mathbf{r}_\ell\cdot \mathbf{c}_n
\end{pmatrix}
$$



<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

We know that the matrix associated with $\psi \circ \varphi$ is:

$$
\begin{align*}
    &\Bigl( \psi \big(\varphi (\mathbf{e}_1) \big) \mid \psi \big(\varphi (\mathbf{e}_2) \big) \mid \dots \mid \psi \big(\varphi (\mathbf{e}_n) \big)\Bigr)\\
    &= \big(\psi (\mathbf{c}_1)\mid \psi(\mathbf{c}_2) \mid \dots \mid \psi(\mathbf{c}_n) \big)
\end{align*}
$$

Now, as $\psi(\mathbf{c}_i) =
\begin{pmatrix}
\mathbf{r}_1\cdot\mathbf{c}_i\\
\mathbf{r}_2\cdot\mathbf{c}_i\\
\vdots\\
\mathbf{r}_\ell\cdot\mathbf{c}_i
\end{pmatrix} $, and so we get the formula successfully.

claude: add a  blacksquare

</details>

:::note

When you do composition of two linear maps you have to multiply the matrices in the opposite order, such that for the linear map $BA\mathbf{x}$, we first compute $A\mathbf{x}$, then apply $B$ to get $B(A\mathbf{x})$

:::
