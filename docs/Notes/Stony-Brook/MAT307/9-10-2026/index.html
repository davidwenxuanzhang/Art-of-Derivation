Binormal Vector​

Binormal Vector

For the curve in R3\mathbb{R}^3R3, the Binormal Vector is:

B(s)≔defT(s)×N(s)\mathbf{B}(s)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{\coloneqq} \mathbf{T}(s)\times \mathbf{N}(s)B(s):=defT(s)×N(s)

B\mathbf{B}B give us the normal direction of the tangent plane to the curve.

Observation

Because T\mathbf{T}T is the tangent unit vector, N\mathbf{N}N is the normal unit vector, and T⊥N\mathbf{T}\perp \mathbf{N}T⊥N:

∥B∥=T(s)×N(s)=∥T(s)∥∥N(s)∥sin⁡90∘=1\|\mathbf{B}\|=\mathbf{T}(s)\times \mathbf{N}(s)=\|\mathbf{T}(s)\|\|\mathbf{N}(s)\|\sin{90^{\circ}}=1∥B∥=T(s)×N(s)=∥T(s)∥∥N(s)∥sin90∘=1
Propositions
ddsB(s)⊥B(s)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{B}(s)\perp \mathbf{B}(s)dsd​B(s)⊥B(s)
Derivation
dds(B(s)⋅B(s)⏟=1 under observation)=0  ⟹  2(ddsB(s)⋅B(s))=0ddsB(s)⋅B(s)=0\begin{align*} \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\big(\underbrace{\mathbf{B}(s) \cdot \mathbf{B}(s) }_{\mathclap{=1\text{ under observation}}}\big)&=0\\ \implies 2\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{B}(s)\cdot \mathbf{B}(s) \right)&=0\\ \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{B}(s)\cdot \mathbf{B}(s)&=0 \tag*{$\blacksquare$} \end{align*}dsd​(=1 under observationB(s)⋅B(s)​​)⟹2(dsd​B(s)⋅B(s))dsd​B(s)⋅B(s)​=0=0=0​■​
ddsB(s)⊥T(s)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{B}(s)\perp \mathbf{T}(s)dsd​B(s)⊥T(s)
Derivation
ddsB(s)=dds(T(s)×N(s))=ddsT(s)×N(s)+ddsN(s)×T(s)=κ(s)N(s)×N(s)⏟0+T(s)×ddsN(s)=T(s)×ddsN(s)\begin{align*} \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{B}(s)&=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\big(\mathbf{T}(s)\times \mathbf{N}(s) \big)\\ &=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{T}(s)\times \mathbf{N}(s) +\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{N}(s) \times \mathbf{T}(s)\\ &=\kappa(s)\underbrace{\mathbf{N}(s)\times \mathbf{N}(s)}_{0}+\mathbf{T}(s)\times \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{N}(s)\\ &=\mathbf{T}(s)\times \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{N}(s)\\ \end{align*}dsd​B(s)​=dsd​(T(s)×N(s))=dsd​T(s)×N(s)+dsd​N(s)×T(s)=κ(s)0N(s)×N(s)​​+T(s)×dsd​N(s)=T(s)×dsd​N(s)​

As T(s)×ddsN(s)\mathbf{T}(s)\times \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{N}(s)T(s)×dsd​N(s) is orthogonal to T(s)\mathbf{T}(s)T(s), therefore:

ddsB(s)⊥T(s)■\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{B}(s)\perp \mathbf{T}(s) \tag*{$\blacksquare$}dsd​B(s)⊥T(s)■

Torsion​

Torsion

A corollary from the binormal vector is:

ddsB(s)=−τN(s)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{B}(s)=-\tau \mathbf{N}(s)dsd​B(s)=−τN(s)

where τ\tauτ is a scalar function called the Torsion of the curve.

Geometric Intuition

Torsion tells us how much the curve is "twisting".

Exercise

Prove the following criterion: A curve γ(s)\gamma (s)γ(s) is a planar if and only if the torsion τ(s)=0\tau(s)=0τ(s)=0 for any sss.

Derivation
  • Only If Direction:

Given a curve γ(s)\gamma (s)γ(s) is planar, we know it has a normal vector k\mathbf{k}k, then:

γ(s)⋅k=Constant\gamma(s)\cdot \mathbf{k}=\text{Constant}γ(s)⋅k=Constant

Take the derivative on both side:

dds(γ(s)⋅k)=0  ⟹  T(s)⋅k=0  ⟹  k⊥T(s)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\big(\gamma(s)\cdot \mathbf{k}\big)=0\implies \mathbf{T}(s)\cdot \mathbf{k}=0 \implies \mathbf{k} \perp \mathbf{T}(s)dsd​(γ(s)⋅k)=0⟹T(s)⋅k=0⟹k⊥T(s)

Take the derivative on both side again:

dds(T(s)⋅k)=κ(s)N(s)⋅k=0  ⟹  k⊥N\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\big(\mathbf{T}(s)\cdot \mathbf{k}\big)=\kappa (s)\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{k}=0\implies \mathbf{k}\perp \mathbf{N}dsd​(T(s)⋅k)=κ(s)N(s)⋅k=0⟹k⊥N

Since B(s)=T(s)×N(s)\mathbf{B}(s)=\mathbf{T}(s) \times \mathbf{N}(s) B(s)=T(s)×N(s), meaning that B(s)⊥T(s)\mathbf{B}(s) \perp \mathbf{T}(s)B(s)⊥T(s) and B(s)⊥N(s)\mathbf{B}(s) \perp \mathbf{N}(s)B(s)⊥N(s). Therefore, the normal vector k\mathbf{k}k is proportional to B(s)\mathbf{B}(s)B(s) with same direction, which implies:

ddsB(s)=0  ⟹  τ(s)N(s)=0  ⟹  τ(s)=0\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{B}(s)=0 \implies \tau(s)\mathbf{N}(s)=0 \implies \tau(s)=0dsd​B(s)=0⟹τ(s)N(s)=0⟹τ(s)=0
  • If Direction:

Assume τ(s)=0\tau(s)=0τ(s)=0, then :

ddsB(s)=−τ(s)N(s)=0  ⟹  B(s) is a constant \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{B}(s)=-\tau(s)\mathbf{N}(s)=0 \implies \mathbf{B}(s) \text{ is a constant}dsd​B(s)=−τ(s)N(s)=0⟹B(s) is a constant

Then, notice the derivative:

dds(γ(s)⋅B(s))′=γ′(s)⋅B(s)+γ(s)⋅B′(s)⏟0(Dot Product Rule)=T(s)⋅B(s)=0(T(s)⊥B(s))\begin{align*} \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\big(\gamma{(s)}\cdot \mathbf{B}(s) \big)^{\prime}&=\gamma^{\prime}(s)\cdot \mathbf{B}(s)+\gamma(s)\cdot \underbrace{\mathbf{B}^{\prime}(s)}_{0} &\qquad \text{(Dot Product Rule)}\\ &=\mathbf{T}(s)\cdot \mathbf{B}(s)\\ &=0 &\qquad (\mathbf{T}(s)\perp \mathbf{B}(s)) \end{align*}dsd​(γ(s)⋅B(s))′​=γ′(s)⋅B(s)+γ(s)⋅0B′(s)​​=T(s)⋅B(s)=0​(Dot Product Rule)(T(s)⊥B(s))​

Hence, γ(s)⋅B(s)\gamma(s)\cdot \mathbf{B}(s)γ(s)⋅B(s) is a constant, which suggests that γ(s)\gamma(s)γ(s) is a plane with normal vector B(s)\mathbf{B}(s)B(s) according to the general form of a plane.

Therefore,

γ(s) is a planar   ⟺  τ(s)=0 for all s■\gamma(s) \text{ is a planar } \iff \tau(s)=0 \text{ for all } s \tag*{$\blacksquare$}γ(s) is a planar ⟺τ(s)=0 for all s■

Criterion κ(s)=0\kappa(s)=0κ(s)=0 if and only if γ(s)\gamma(s)γ(s) lie on a line.

Derivation

Frenet–Serret Formula​

We start with a leading question:

Question

Compute ddsN(s)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s) dsd​N(s) in terms of κ(s),τ(s),T(s),and B(s)\kappa(s), \tau(s), \mathbf{T}(s), \text{and } \mathbf{B}(s) κ(s),τ(s),T(s),and B(s)

Solution

Because T,N,B\mathbf{T}, \mathbf{N}, \mathbf{B}T,N,B construct a orthogonal basis at any point, so any vectors in R3\mathbb{R}^3R3, including ddsN(s)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)dsd​N(s), could be written as:

ddsN(s)=a(s)T(s)+b(s)N(s)+c(s)B(s)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)= a(s) \mathbf{T}(s)+b(s)\mathbf{N}(s)+c(s)\mathbf{B}(s)dsd​N(s)=a(s)T(s)+b(s)N(s)+c(s)B(s)

Also, because T,N,B\mathbf{T}, \mathbf{N}, \mathbf{B}T,N,B are unit vectors and orthogonal to each other:

a(s)=ddsN(s)⋅T(s)b(s)=ddsN(s)⋅N(s)c(s)=ddsN(s)⋅B(s)\begin{align*} a(s)&=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{T}(s)\\ b(s)&=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{N}(s)\\ c(s)&=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{B}(s) \end{align*}a(s)b(s)c(s)​=dsd​N(s)⋅T(s)=dsd​N(s)⋅N(s)=dsd​N(s)⋅B(s)​

As N(s)\mathbf{N}(s)N(s) is a unit vector:

N(s)⋅N(s)=1dds(N(s)⋅N(s))=0ddsN(s)⋅N(s)=0  ⟹  b(s)=0\begin{align*} \mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{N}(s)&=1\\ \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\big(\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{N}(s) \big)&=0\\ \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{N}(s)&=0 \implies b(s)=0 \end{align*}N(s)⋅N(s)dsd​(N(s)⋅N(s))dsd​N(s)⋅N(s)​=1=0=0⟹b(s)=0​

Therefore:

ddsN(s)=a(s)T(s)+c(s)B(s)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)= a(s) \mathbf{T}(s)+c(s)\mathbf{B}(s)dsd​N(s)=a(s)T(s)+c(s)B(s)

From T(s)⋅N(s)=0\mathbf{T}(s)\cdot \mathbf{N}(s)=0T(s)⋅N(s)=0 and :

dds(T(s)⋅N(s))=0ddsT(s)⋅N(s)+ddsN(s)⋅T(s)=0(Dot Product Rule)a(s)=ddsN(s)⋅T(s)=−ddsT(s)⋅N(s)\begin{align*} \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\big( \mathbf{T}(s)\cdot \mathbf{N}(s) \big)&=0\\ \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{T}(s)\cdot \mathbf{N}(s)+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{T}(s)&=0 &\qquad \text{(Dot Product Rule)}\\ a(s)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{T}(s)&=-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{T}(s)\cdot \mathbf{N}(s)\\ \end{align*}dsd​(T(s)⋅N(s))dsd​T(s)⋅N(s)+dsd​N(s)⋅T(s)a(s)=dsd​N(s)⋅T(s)​=0=0=−dsd​T(s)⋅N(s)​(Dot Product Rule)​

Given the fact that ddsT(s)=−κ(s)N(s)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{T}(s)= - \kappa(s) \mathbf{N}(s)dsd​T(s)=−κ(s)N(s)

a(s)=ddsN(s)⋅T(s)=−ddsT(s)⋅N(s)=−κ(s)N(s)⋅N(s)=−κ(s)(N(s)⋅N(s))=−κ(s)\begin{align*} a(s)= \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{T}(s)&=-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{T}(s)\cdot \mathbf{N}(s)\\ &=- \kappa (s)\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{N}(s)\\ &=-\kappa(s) \big(\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot {\mathbf{N}(s)} \big)\\ &=-\kappa(s) \end{align*}a(s)=dsd​N(s)⋅T(s)​=−dsd​T(s)⋅N(s)=−κ(s)N(s)⋅N(s)=−κ(s)(N(s)⋅N(s))=−κ(s)​

From N(s)⋅B(s)=0\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{B}(s)=0N(s)⋅B(s)=0

dds(N(s)⋅B(s))=0ddsN(s)⋅B(s)+ddsB(s)⋅N(s)=0(Dot Product Rule)c(s)=ddsN(s)⋅B(s)=−ddsB(s)⋅N(s)\begin{align*} \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\big( \mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{B}(s) \big)&=0\\ \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{B}(s)+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{B}(s)\cdot \mathbf{N}(s)&=0 &\qquad \text{(Dot Product Rule)}\\ c(s)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{B}(s)&=-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{B}(s)\cdot \mathbf{N}(s)\\ \end{align*}dsd​(N(s)⋅B(s))dsd​N(s)⋅B(s)+dsd​B(s)⋅N(s)c(s)=dsd​N(s)⋅B(s)​=0=0=−dsd​B(s)⋅N(s)​(Dot Product Rule)​

Given the fact that ddsB(s)=−τ(s)N(s)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{B}(s)= - \tau (s)\mathbf{N}(s)dsd​B(s)=−τ(s)N(s)

c(s)=ddsN(s)⋅B(s)=−ddsB(s)⋅N(s)=−τ(s)N(s)⋅N(s)=−τ(s)(N(s)⋅N(s))=−τ(s)\begin{align*} c(s)= \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{B}(s)&=-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{B}(s)\cdot \mathbf{N}(s)\\ &=- \tau (s)\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot \mathbf{N}(s)\\ &=-\tau (s)\big(\mathbf{N}(s)\cdot {\mathbf{N}(s)} \big)\\ &=-\tau(s) \end{align*}c(s)=dsd​N(s)⋅B(s)​=−dsd​B(s)⋅N(s)=−τ(s)N(s)⋅N(s)=−τ(s)(N(s)⋅N(s))=−τ(s)​

Hence:

ddsN(s)=κ(s)T(s)+τ(s)B(s)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)= \kappa (s)\mathbf{T}(s)+\tau(s)\mathbf{B}(s)dsd​N(s)=κ(s)T(s)+τ(s)B(s)

This leads to the Frenet–Serret Formula

Frenet–Serret Formula

Given the unit tangent, normal, and binormal vector T(s),N(s),B(s)\mathbf{T}(s),\mathbf{N}(s),\mathbf{B}(s)T(s),N(s),B(s) of a curve, we have:

ddsT(s)=κ(s)N(s)ddsN(s)=κ(s)T(s)+τ(s)B(s)ddsB(s)=τ(s)N(s)\begin{align*} \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{T}(s)&= \kappa (s)\mathbf{N}(s)\\ \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{N}(s)&= \kappa (s)\mathbf{T}(s)+\tau(s)\mathbf{B}(s)\\ \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}{s}}\mathbf{B}(s)&= \tau (s)\mathbf{N}(s)\\ \end{align*}dsd​T(s)dsd​N(s)dsd​B(s)​=κ(s)N(s)=κ(s)T(s)+τ(s)B(s)=τ(s)N(s)​

where κ(s)\kappa(s)κ(s) is the curvature curvature, and τ(s)\tau (s)τ(s) is the torsion.

note

These equations determine the shape of the curve up to an isometry.
