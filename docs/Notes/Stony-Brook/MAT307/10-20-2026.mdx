## General Hessian Criterion

::::tip General Hessian Criterion

For a function $f: \mathbb{R}^n\rightarrow \mathbb{R}$, we take $p\in \mathbb{R}^n$ such that $\nabla {f(p)}=0$, and this is the necessary condition for being local max/min.

At any point in $\mathbb{R}^n$ you can construct a Hessian Matrix, as:

$$
\operatorname{Hess}(f)(p)=
\begin{bmatrix}
\partial_{x_1}^2 f(p) & \cdots & \partial_{x_n}\partial_{x_1}f(p)\\
\vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\
\partial_{x_1}\partial_{x_n}f(p) & \cdots & \partial_{x_n}^2 f(p)
\end{bmatrix}
$$
We can look at $\mathbf{d}=(d_1,d_2,\dots,d_n)$, where $d_i$ is called **Leading Principle Minors** that gives the determinant of top-left matrix with $i$ row/column.

$$
d_1=\partial_{x_1}^{2}f(p)\qquad d_2=\operatorname{det}
\begin{bmatrix}
\partial_{x_1}^2 f(p) & \partial_{x_1}\partial_{x_2}f(p)\\
\partial_{x_2}\partial_{x_1}f(p) & \partial_{x_2}^2 f(p)
\end{bmatrix}

$$

$$
d_i=\operatorname{det}\begin{bmatrix}
\partial_{x_1}^2 f(p) & \cdots & \partial_{x_i}\partial_{x_1}f(p)\\
\vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\
\partial_{x_1}\partial_{x_i}f(p) & \cdots & \partial_{x_i}^2 f(p)
\end{bmatrix}
$$

-  If $d_i>0$ for any $i$, then $p$ is a local minimum

-  If $d_1<0, d_2>0,\dots,d_n $ has opposite sign with respect to $d_{n-1}$ for any $i$, then $p$ is a local maximum 

-  If $d_n\ne 0$ and neither the above hold, then $p$ is a saddle point.
-  If $d_n=0$, then the Hessian Criterion is inconclusive

<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

The $3$ conditions above control the sign of the degree $2$ polynomial:

$$
T_f^{(2)} (p)(\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_n)-f(p)=\sum_{i,j}\partial_{x_i}\partial_{x_j}f(p)\varepsilon_i \varepsilon_j
$$

</details>

:::info Example
Given a function, we are looking for its local extrema:
$$
f(x,y,z)=x^2+y^2+z^2+xy+yz
$$

Find the critical point:
$$
\nabla f(x,y,z)=(2x+y,x+2y+z,2z+y)=0\iff (x,y,z)=(0,0,0)
$$
Find the Hessian Matrix:
$$
\operatorname{Hess}(f)(x,y,z)=
\begin{bmatrix}
2 & 1 & 0\\
1 & 2 & 1\\
0 & 1 & 2
\end{bmatrix}
$$
Use the General Hessian Criterion:
$$
d_1=2\qquad d_2=\operatorname{det} 
\begin{bmatrix}
2 & 1\\
1 & 2
\end{bmatrix}
= 4 - 1 = 3 
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
d_3 = \operatorname{det}  \begin{bmatrix}
2 & 1 & 0\\
1 & 2 & 1\\
0 & 1 & 2
\end{bmatrix}
&= 2\operatorname{det} 
\begin{bmatrix}
2 & 1\\
1 & 2
\end{bmatrix}
- 1\operatorname{det} 
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 1\\
0 & 2
\end{bmatrix}
- 0\\
&=2\cdot 3-1\cdot 2=4
\end{align*}
$$
$$
\mathbf{d}=(2,3,4)\implies d_i \text{'s are positive}
$$

$\implies (0,0,0)$ is a local minimum (in this case is also global minimum).

:::

:::danger Remark

Why Hessian Test is inconclusive if $d_n=0$

Morally, if $d_n=0$, then the sign of $f(x)-f(p)$ is not controlled by $\sum_{i,j}\partial_{x_i}\partial_{x_j}f(p)\varepsilon_i \varepsilon_j$

:::info Example

$$
f(x,y,z)=x^2+y^2+xy+z\implies d_3=0 \text{ and } (0,0,0)\text{ is a local min}
$$


$$
g(x,y,z)=x^2+y^2+xy-z\implies d_3=0 \text{ and } (0,0,0)\text{ is not a local min}
$$

:::

## Considered Domain

So far we always supposed that $f:\mathbb{R}^n\rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ was defined over some open $D$.

More precisely, we are only looking for min/max over an **open subset** $D\subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$

::::tip Open Set 

$D\subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ is open if for every point $p\in D$ there is a small ball $B_p(\varepsilon)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^n\|x-p \|<\varepsilon  \}\subseteq D$ for $\varepsilon > 0$

## Lagrangian Multiples


::::tip Lagrangian Multiples

Let $f:\mathbb{R}^n\rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ be a function and the domain as $D$, which is the level set of a function $g$, that is, $D=\{\mathbf{x}\in \mathbb{R}^n\text{ with }g(\mathbf{x})=k \}$

Suppose that $\nabla g(\mathbf{x})\ne 0 \text{ for all } \mathbf{x}\in D $

If $p\in D$ is a local min/max for $f$ over $D$, then $\nabla f(p)$ is parallel to $\nabla g(p)$, that is to say, $\nabla f(p)= \lambda \nabla g(p)$ for some $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$
