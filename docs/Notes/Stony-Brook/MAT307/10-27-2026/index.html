Vector Fields​

Notation
u:Rn⟶Rn or F:Rn⟶Rn\mathbf{u}: \mathbb{R}^n\longrightarrow \mathbb{R}^n \text{ or }\mathbf{F}: \mathbb{R}^n\longrightarrow \mathbb{R}^n u:Rn⟶Rn or F:Rn⟶Rn
Example
  • Physical Example: force field is a vector field, the same as a magnetic field.
  • Important Example: the gradient of a function ∇f=u\nabla f=\mathbf{u}∇f=u is a vector field.
  • Gravitational Field: gravitational field can be expressed as:
F(r)=−Gr∥r∥3\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r})=\frac{-G\mathbf{r}}{\|\mathbf{r} \|^3}F(r)=∥r∥3−Gr​

Integral of a Vector Field Along a Curve​

Integral of a Vector Field Along a Curve (Flow)

Given a curve γ\gammaγ:

γ:[a,b]⟶Rn\gamma : [a,b]\longrightarrow \mathbb{R}^nγ:[a,b]⟶Rn

Let CCC be the image of the curve in Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn (forgetting the parametrization), then the integral of a vector field along a curve, also known as flow, is defined as:

∫CF⋅dr≔∫abF(γ(t))⋅γ′(t) dt\int_{C}\mathbf{F}\cdot \mathrm{d}r \coloneqq \int_{a}^{b}\mathbf{F}\big(\gamma (t) \big)\cdot \gamma^{\prime}(t)\ \mathrm{d}t∫C​F⋅dr:=∫ab​F(γ(t))⋅γ′(t) dt
Remark

It does not depend on the choice of a parametrization (we saw it last time) provided that we choose a parametrization going through the curve once from A=γ(a)A=\gamma(a)A=γ(a) and B=γ(b)B=\gamma(b)B=γ(b)

In general, choosing t=t(s)t=t(s)t=t(s) for an increasing differentiable function t(s)t(s)t(s) works.

Note

When writing C⊆RnC\subseteq \mathbb{R}^nC⊆Rn, I am thinking of a curve together with a direction of travel (we will refer to it as an oriented curve)

Side Remark

If F\mathbf{F}F is a force vector field, then ∫CF⋅dr\int_{C}\mathbf{F}\cdot \mathrm{d}r∫C​F⋅dr is the work that the force F\mathbf{F}F does on a particle traveling through CCC.

Question

Given a vector field u\mathbf{u}u, when can we write u=∇g\mathbf{u}=\nabla gu=∇g?

Potential

If such ggg exists, it is called a Potential of u\mathbf{u}u.

Derivation

Let's try to find some conditions that u\mathbf{u}u must satisfy in order to admit a potential.

  • Suppose that u=∇g\mathbf{u}=\nabla gu=∇g for some g:Rn→Rg:\mathbb{R}^n \rightarrow \mathbb{R}g:Rn→R

Consider a curve CCC with a parametrization γ\gammaγ, what can I say about

∫Cu⋅dr=∫abu(γ(t))⋅γ′(t) dt=∫ab∇g(γ(t))⋅γ′(t) dt\begin{align*} \int_{C}\mathbf{u}\cdot \mathrm{d}r &= \int_{a}^{b}\mathbf{u}\big(\gamma(t) \big)\cdot \gamma^{\prime}(t)\ \mathrm{d}t\\ &=\int_{a}^{b}\nabla g\big(\gamma(t) \big)\cdot \gamma^{\prime}(t)\ \mathrm{d}t \end{align*}∫C​u⋅dr​=∫ab​u(γ(t))⋅γ′(t) dt=∫ab​∇g(γ(t))⋅γ′(t) dt​

Notice that, by the Chain Rule, ∇g(t)⋅γ′(t)=g′(γ(t))=ddtg(γ(t))\nabla g (t) \cdot \gamma^{\prime}(t)=g^{\prime}\big(\gamma(t) \big)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t} g\big(\gamma(t) \big) ∇g(t)⋅γ′(t)=g′(γ(t))=dtd​g(γ(t)); by the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus:

∫ab∇g(γ(t))⋅γ′(t) dt=g(γ(t))∣ab=g(γ(b))−g(γ(a))=g(B)−g(A)\int_{a}^{b}\nabla g\big(\gamma(t) \big)\cdot \gamma^{\prime}(t)\ \mathrm{d}t=g\big(\gamma(t)\big)\Big|_{a}^{b}=g\big(\gamma(b)\big)-g\big(\gamma(a)\big)=g(B)-g(A)∫ab​∇g(γ(t))⋅γ′(t) dt=g(γ(t))​ab​=g(γ(b))−g(γ(a))=g(B)−g(A)
Corollary 1

u=∇g  ⟹  ∫Cu⋅dr=g(B)−g(A)\mathbf{u}=\nabla g\implies \int_{C}\mathbf{u}\cdot \mathrm{d}r=g(B)-g(A)u=∇g⟹∫C​u⋅dr=g(B)−g(A) depends only on the start and end point of the curve!

Corollary 2

u=∇g  ⟹  ∫Cu⋅dr=0\mathrm{u}=\nabla g\implies \int_{C}\mathbf{u}\cdot \mathrm{d}r=0u=∇g⟹∫C​u⋅dr=0 for any closed curve CCC, that is to say, A=BA=BA=B.

More importantly, by taking the contrapositive of this corollary: if I can find a closed curve CCC such that ∫CF⋅dr≠0\int_{C}\mathbf{F}\cdot \mathrm{d}r\ne 0 ∫C​F⋅dr=0, then FFF is not a gradient (does not admit a potential).

Conservative Vector Field​

Theorem

Given a vector field u\mathbf{u}u, the following are equivalent:

  1. ∫Cu⋅dr=0\int_{C}\mathbf{u}\cdot \mathrm{d}r=0∫C​u⋅dr=0 for any closed curve CCC
  2. u\mathbf{u}u admits a potential ggg.
