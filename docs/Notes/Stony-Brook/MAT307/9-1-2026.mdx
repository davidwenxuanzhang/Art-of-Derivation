## Cross Product

::::tip Cross Product

Take two vectors $\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{w}\in \mathbb{R}^3$, and ti gives us another vector $\mathbf{v}\times \mathbf{w}$

<div
  style={{
    display: "grid",
    gridTemplateColumns: "1fr 1fr",
    gap: "0",
  }}
>
  <div
    style={{
      paddingRight: "2rem",
      borderRight: "1px solid var(--ifm-color-emphasis-300)",
    }}
  >

**Geometrically**

Vector $\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{w}$ is the unique vector satisfying that $\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{w}$ is orthogonal to both $\mathbf{v}$ and $\mathbf{w}$

The magnitude of $\mathbf{v}\times \mathbf{w}$ is given by:

$$
\|\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{w}\|=\|\mathbf{v}\|\|\mathbf{w}\|\sin{\theta}
$$

The orientation is decided by the right hand rule or look at the the sign of $\sin{\theta}$ that it is up if $\sin{\theta}>0$ and down if $\sin{\theta}< 0$

  </div>

  <div
    style={{
      paddingLeft: "2rem",
    }}
  >

**Analytically**


For two vectors:

$$
\mathbf{v}=(v_1,v_2,v_3) \qquad \mathbf{w}=(w_1,w_2,w_3)
$$

Their cross product: 

$$
\begin{align*}
    \mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{w}&=\begin{vmatrix}
\mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\
v_1 & v_2 & v_3 \\
w_1 & w_2 & w_3
\end{vmatrix}\\
                              &=(v_2w_3-w_2v_3,v_3w_1-v_1w_3,v_1w_2-v_2w_1)          
\end{align*}
$$

  </div>
</div>

Now we want to prove that the analytic and geometric definition agrees, below are some useful properties:

:::info Properties
- $\mathbf{v}\times \mathbf{w}=-\mathbf{w}\times \mathbf{v}$
- $\mathbf{v}\times (\mathbf{w}_1+\mathbf{w}_2)=\mathbf{v}\times \mathbf{w}_1+\mathbf{v}\times \mathbf{w}_2$
- $\mathbf{v}\times c\mathbf{w}=c(\mathbf{v}\times \mathbf{w})$
:::

## Curves

::::tip Curves

For us, a parametric curve is a vecctor function with triples of variables:

$$
\gamma: \underset{(a;b)}{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}^3}
$$
Then, a parameter $t\in (a,b)$ is send to a point in 3D:
$$
t\mapsto \gamma (t)=\big(x(t),y(t),z(t)\big)
$$

:::info Example 1

For general form of a curve:

$$
\begin{align*}
    \gamma (t)&=P_0+\mathbf{v}t\\
              &=(x_0,y_0,z_0)+t(v_1,v_2,v_3)\\
              &=(x_0+tv_1,y_0+tv_2,z_0+tv_3)\\
\end{align*}
$$
For $t=1$, we have $\gamma (1)=P_0+\mathbf{v}$.

As $t$ varies in $\mathbb{R}$, we describes all the points on the line.
:::

### Differentiation

::::tip Differentiation

We say that a curve $\gamma(t)=\big(x(t),y(t),z(t) \big) $ is differentiable if  $x(t)$, $y(t)$, and $z(t)$ are differentiable. 

Then, we define  $\gamma^{\prime}(t)=\big(x(t)^{\prime},y(t)^{\prime},z(t)^{\prime} \big)$

$\gamma^{\prime}(t_0)$ is tangent to the curve at the point $\gamma(t_0)$ (i.e. the image of $\gamma$ in $\mathbb{R}$)

:::info Properties 

$$ 
\begin{align*} 
  1. &\big(\gamma_1(t)+\gamma_2{(t)} \big)^{\prime}=\gamma_1^{\prime}(t)+\gamma_2^{\prime}(t) &\qquad \text{()}\\
  2. &\big(k\gamma{(t)} \big)^{\prime}=k\gamma^{\prime}(t) &\qquad \text{()}\\
  3. &\big(\gamma_1(t)\cdot \gamma_2{(t)} \big)^{\prime}=\gamma_1^{\prime}(t)\cdot\gamma_2(t)+\gamma_2^{\prime}(t)\cdot\gamma_1(t) &\qquad \text{(Dot Product Rule)}\\
  4. &\big(\gamma_1(t)\times \gamma_2{(t)} \big)^{\prime}=\gamma_1^{\prime}(t)\times\gamma_2(t)+\gamma_2^{\prime}(t)\times\gamma_1(t) &\qquad \text{(Cross Product Rule)}\\ 
  5. &\big(k(t)\gamma{(t)} \big)^{\prime}=k^{\prime}(t)\gamma(t)+k(t)\gamma^{\prime}(t)  &\qquad \text{(Product by Scalar)}\\
  6. &\Bigl(\gamma \big( t(s)\big)  \Bigl)^{\prime}=\gamma^{\prime} \big(t(s) \big)t^\prime(s)&\qquad \text{(Chain Rule)}
\end{align*} 
$$
