## Functions of Several Variables

:::tip Functions of Several Variables

Functions of several variables is: 

$$
f: \mathbb{R}^n\rightarrow \mathbb{R}
$$

More generally, the domain $D$ could just be a subset of $\mathbb{R}$ as:

$$
f:D\rightarrow \mathbb{R}, \qquad D\subseteq \mathbb{R}^n
$$


:::

### Graph of a Function

Graph is a really convinient way to think of a function of several variables, here we will do a example:

#### Paraboloid

:::info Example (Paraboloid)

Consider the function:

$$
f(x,y)=x^2+y^2
$$

this is a case where $n=2$, and we can try to graph it:

$$
\operatorname{graph}(f)= \{(x,y,z)\in \mathbb{R}^3 \text{ such that } z= f(x,y) \}
$$

### Vertical Slice

But, given a graph, how can we determine whether or not it is a paraboloid? Vertical Slice is a good method.

::::tip Vertical Slice

I can slice the graph with vertical plane that contains $z$-axis and the normal direction is on the $x,y$ plane; then, let the normal vector be $a,b,0$, the plane could be written as:

$$
ax+by=0  
\begin{cases}
y=-\frac{a}{b}x\qquad \text{if }b\ne 0\\
x=-\frac{b}{a}y\qquad \text{if }a\ne 0
\end{cases}
$$

then, the equation of the function on such plane looks like:

:::danger Remark

Here we are distorting the slice slightly, so we may want to normalize it. For example, in the previous example, we slice $y=2x$ and get $z=5x^2$, so the normalized slice is:

$$
(x,y)=t\mathbf{v}=t\frac{(1,2)}{\sqrt{5}}=\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{5}},\frac{2t}{\sqrt{5}}\right)
$$
$$
z=f(t\mathbf{v})= \frac{t^2}{5}+\frac{4t^2}{5}=t^2
$$

### Level Sets

::::tip Level Sets

Given a function: 

$$
f:\mathbb{R}^n\rightarrow \mathbb{R}
$$

a **level set** for $f$ is:

$$
\ell_{k}=\{ \mathbf{x}\in \mathbb{R}^n \text{ such that } f(\mathbf{x})=k \}
$$


:::info Example

Back to the example [paraboloid](#paraboloid),

$$
f(x,y)=x^2+y^2
$$
$$
\ell_{k}=\{ \mathbf{x}\in \mathbb{R}^2 \text{ such that } x^2+y^2=k \}
$$
