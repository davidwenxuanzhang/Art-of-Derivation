## De Morgan's Law

:::tip De Morgan's Law 
For two events $A$ and $B$, then
$$
(A\cup B)^{c}=A^{c}\cap B^{c}
$$
$$
(A\cap B)^{c}=A^{c}\cup B^{c}
$$

## Complement Rule {#Complement-Rule}

:::tip Complement Rule

For any event $A$:
$$
\mathbb{P}(A^{c})=1-\mathbb{P}(A)
$$
<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

From the definition of Complement, we know that $A$ and $A^{c}$ are mutually exclusive, and they form the entire sample space, as:
$$
A\cup A^{c}=\Omega
$$
By [Countable Additivity](docs\Statistics\Probability-Theory\Probability-Basics.mdx#Probability-Axioms):
$$
\mathbb{P}(A\cup A^{c})=\mathbb{P}(A)+\mathbb{P}(A^{c})=\mathbb{P}(\Omega) \overset{\text{Normalization}}{=} 1
$$
Hence:
$$
\mathbb{P}(A^{c})=1-\mathbb{P}(A)  \tag*{$\blacksquare$}
$$
</details>
:::


## Probability of the Empty Set
:::tip Probability of the Empty Set

The empty event has probability zero, as:

$$
\mathbb{P}(\varnothing)=0
$$
<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

Aa $\Omega$ represent all possible outcome, the complement of it according to definition is $\varnothing$, as:

$$
\Omega^{c}=\varnothing
$$

By [Complement Rule](#Complement-Rule)：
$$
\mathbb{P}(\varnothing)=1-\mathbb{P}(\Omega)\overset{\text{Normalization}}{=}1-1=0 \tag*{$\blacksquare$}
$$

</details>
:::

## Probability Bounds

:::tip Probability Bounds 

For any event $A$, probability is between 0 and 1, as:

$$
0\le \mathbb{P}(A) \le 1
$$

<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

By the [Non-negativity Axiom](docs\Statistics\Probability-Theory\Probability-Basics.mdx#Probability-Axioms):

$$
0\le \mathbb{P}(A)
$$
Also, by the [Complement Rule](#Complement-Rule):

$$
\mathbb{P}(A^{c})=1-\mathbb{P}(A)
$$

Since probabilities are non-negative:
$$
\mathbb{P}(A^{c})\ge 0
$$
Therefore: 
$$
1-\mathbb{P}(A)\ge 0 \implies \mathbb{P}(A) \le 1
$$

Hence: 
$$
0\le \mathbb{P}(A) \le 1  \tag*{$\blacksquare$}
$$
</details>
:::

## Subset Rule

:::tip Subset Rule

For any event that $A$ $\subseteq$ $B$: 

$$
\mathbb{P}(A)\le \mathbb{P}(B)
$$
<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

If $A$ $\subseteq$ $B$, then event $B$ could split into two mutually exclusive parts, one with event $A$ and one without, as:

$$
B=A\cup (B\cap A^{c} )
$$
where

$$
A\cap (B\cap A^{c} )=\varnothing
$$

By [Countable Additivity](docs\Statistics\Probability-Theory\Probability-Basics.mdx#Probability-Axioms):
$$
\mathbb{P}(B)=\mathbb{P}(A\cup (B\cap A^{c}) )=\mathbb{P}(A)+\mathbb{P}(B\cap A^{c} )
$$

Also, by the [Non-negativity Axiom](docs\Statistics\Probability-Theory\Probability-Basics.mdx#Probability-Axioms):

$$
\mathbb{P}(B\cap A^{c} ) \ge 0
$$

Therefore: 
$$
\mathbb{P}(B)=\mathbb{P}(A)+\mathbb{P}(B\cap A^{c}) \ge \mathbb{P}(A)
$$

Hence:
$$
\mathbb{P}(A)\le \mathbb{P}(B) \tag*{$\blacksquare$}
$$
</details>
:::

## Addition Rule

:::tip Addition Rule

For any two events $A$ and $B$:

$$
\mathbb{P}(A\cup B)=\mathbb{P}(A)+\mathbb{P}(B)-\mathbb{P}(A\cap B)
$$

<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

For the event $A\cup B$, it could split into two mutually exclusive parts, one with event $A$ and one with event $B$ but not with $A$, as: 

$$
A\cup B= A\cup (B\cap A^{c})
$$

where

$$
A\cap (B\cap A^{c} )=\varnothing
$$

By [Countable Additivity](docs\Statistics\Probability-Theory\Probability-Basics.mdx#Probability-Axioms):
$$
\mathbb{P}(A\cup B)= \mathbb{P}(A)+\mathbb{P}(B\cap A^{c}) \tag ①
$$

For the event $B$, it could also split into two mutually exclusive parts, one with event $A$ and one without, as: 
$$
B=(A\cap B)\cup ( B\cap  A^{c})
$$

And because these two parts are also mutually exclusive, again, by [Countable Additivity](docs\Statistics\Probability-Theory\Probability-Basics.mdx#Probability-Axioms):
$$
\mathbb{P}(B)=\mathbb{P}(A\cap B)+\mathbb{P}( B\cap  A^{c})
$$
$$
\mathbb{P}( B\cap  A^{c})=\mathbb{P}(B)-\mathbb{P}(A\cap B)
$$

Plug in back to ①:

$$
A\cup B= \mathbb{P}(A)+\mathbb{P}(B\cap A^{c})=  \mathbb{P}(A)+\mathbb{P}(B)-\mathbb{P}(A\cap B)  \tag*{$\blacksquare$}
$$
</details>

:::

## Union Bound

:::tip Union Bound

For any two events $A$ and $B$:

$$
\mathbb{P}(A\cup B)\le \mathbb{P}(A)+\mathbb{P}(B)
$$

Generally, for a collection of events $A_1, \cdots, A_n$: 

$$
\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}A_i\right)\le \sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(A_i)
$$

## Inclusion-Exclusion Principle 

:::tip Inclusion-Exclusion Principle 

For a collection of events $A_1, \cdots, A_n$:
$$
\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}A_i\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(A_i)-\sum_{1\le i\le j\le n} \mathbb{P}(A_i\cap A_j)+\sum_{1\le i\le j\le k \le n} \mathbb{P}(A_i\cap A_j\cap A_k)-\cdots + (-1)^{n+1}\mathbb{P}(A_1\cap \cdots \cap\ A_n)
