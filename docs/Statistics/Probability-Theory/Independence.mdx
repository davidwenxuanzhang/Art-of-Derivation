Literally, when say that two events are independence means the result of one will not influence another. But in the Probability Theory, we need to define it in a mathematical way.

## Definition of Independence   

:::tip Independence

Two events $A$ and $B$ with positive probability are independence if either:
$$
P(A\mid B)=P(A)
$$
        or 
$$
P(B\mid A)=P(B)
$$
:::

With this, we can simplify the  [multiplication rule](docs\Statistics\Probability-Theory\Conditional-Probability.mdx#multiplication-rule):

:::tip Independence 

Two events $A$ and $B$ with positive probability are independence if and only if::

$$
P(A\cap B)=P(A) P(B)
$$

<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

According to the [multiplication rule](docs\Statistics\Probability-Theory\Conditional-Probability.mdx#multiplication-rule) that says: 

$$
P(A\cap B)=P(A) P(B\mid A)
$$

- "Only if": Suppose $A$ and $B$ are independence, we have: $P(B\mid A)=P(B)$; substituting the result of [multiplication rule](docs\Statistics\Probability-Theory\Conditional-Probability.mdx#multiplication-rule), we have:


$$
P(A\cap B)=P(A) P(B\mid A)=P(A) P(B)
$$

- "If": Suppose $P(A\cap B)=P(A) P(B)$; substituting the result of [multiplication rule](docs\Statistics\Probability-Theory\Conditional-Probability.mdx#multiplication-rule), we have:

$$
P(A) P(B)=P(A)P(B\mid A)
$$
Dividing both side by $P(A)$:

$$
P(B)=P(B\mid A) 

$$
<div style={{ display: "flex", justifyContent: "space-between", alignItems: "center" }}>
And we get the result showing $A$ and $B$ are independence.

$\blacksquare$
</div>
</details>

:::

## Conditional Independence

:::tip Conditional Independence

A collection of positive probability events $A_1, A_2,\dots, A_n$ is conditional independent to the event $B$ (where $P(B)>0$) given $\widetilde{P}(A)=P(A\mid B)$

$$
\widetilde{P}(A_{i1}\cap A_{i2}\cap \cdots \cap A_{in})=\widetilde{P}(A_{i1})\widetilde{P}(A_{i2})\cdots \widetilde{P}(A_{in})
$$
or

$$
P(A_{i1}\cap A_{i2}\cap \cdots \cap A_{in} \mid B)= \widetilde{P}(A_{i1}\mid B)\widetilde{P}(A_{i2}\mid B)\cdots \widetilde{P}(A_{in}\mid B)
$$
:::
