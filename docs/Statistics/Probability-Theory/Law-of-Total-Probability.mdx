## Law of Total Probability (LoTP) {#LoTP}

Before getting into the Law of Total Probability, we need to understand the following definition：

:::tip Partition

In the set theory, a partition is the disjoint event $B_1, B_2, \dots $ that cover the entire sample space $\Omega$:

- Disjoint 

$$
B_i\cap B_j=\varnothing
$$

- Collectively Exaustive (cover the entire sample space $\Omega$)

$$
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}A_i=\Omega
$$

:::

:::tip Law of Total Probability (LoTP)

Consider a collection of events $B_1, B_2,\dots$ with positive probability that partition the sample space $\Omega$. For any event $A$, the Law of Total Probability says:

$$
\mathbb{P}(A)=\sum_{i}\mathbb{P}(B_i)\mathbb{P}(A\mid B_i)
$$

<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

Because the sets $A\cap B_i$ are mutually exclusive, and their union is $A$; therefore, by [Countable Additivity](docs\Statistics\Probability-Theory\Probability-Basics.mdx#Probability-Axioms): 

$$
\mathbb{P}(A)=\sum_{i}\mathbb{P}(A\cap B_i)
$$
By the [Multiplication Rule](docs\Statistics\Probability-Theory\Conditional-Probability.mdx#multiplication-rule), the part inside the sum could simplify to:

$$
\mathbb{P}(A\cap B_i)=\mathbb{P}(A)\mathbb{P}(A\mid B_i)
$$
Substituting back to the sum above: 

$$
\mathbb{P}(A)=\sum_{i}\mathbb{P}(A)\mathbb{P}(A\mid B_i) \tag*{$\blacksquare$}

$$

</details>

:::
