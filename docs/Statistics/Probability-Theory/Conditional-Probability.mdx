## Definition of Conditional Probability {#conditional-probability}

:::tip Conditional Probability

For two events $A$ and $B$ with $\mathbb{P}(B)>0$, the **Conditional Probabilty** of event $A$ happens given that event $B$ has happened is defined as:

$$
\mathbb{P}(A\mid B)\overset{\text{def}}{=}\frac{\mathbb{P}(A\cap B)}{\mathbb{P}(B)}
$$

:::

## Multiplication Rule {#multiplication-rule}

:::tip Multiplication Rule for Two Event

If $A$ and $B$ are two events with $\mathbb{P}(A)>0$ and $\mathbb{P}(B)>0$, then the multiplcation rule says: 

$$
\begin{align*}
\mathbb{P}(A\cap B)&=\mathbb{P}(A) \mathbb{P}(B\mid A)\\
          &= \mathbb{P}(B) \mathbb{P}(A\mid B)
\end{align*}
$$

<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

According to the definition of [conditional probability](docs\Statistics\Probability-Theory\Conditional-Probability.mdx#conditional-probability):

$$
\mathbb{P}(A\mid B)\overset{\text{def}}{=}\frac{\mathbb{P}(A\cap B)}{\mathbb{P}(B)}
$$

Multiplying both sides by $\mathbb{P}(B)$ we get:

$$
\mathbb{P}(A\cap B)=\mathbb{P}(A) \mathbb{P}(B\mid A)
$$

Similarly from $\mathbb{P}(B\mid A)$ we may also get: 

$$
\mathbb{P}(A\cap B)=\mathbb{P}(B) \mathbb{P}(A\mid B)
$$

Hence: 

$$
\begin{align*}
\mathbb{P}(A\cap B)&=\mathbb{P}(A) \mathbb{P}(B\mid A)\\
          &= \mathbb{P}(B) \mathbb{P}(A\mid B)   \tag*{$\blacksquare$}
\end{align*}
$$
</details>
:::

We can generalize this multiplcation rule to more events; first, we can simplify the notation of intersection as :

$$
\mathbb{P}(A_1\cap A_2 \cap \cdots \cap A_n)\overset{\text{def}}{=}\mathbb{P}(A_1,A_2,\cdots, A_n)
$$

where comma inside could read as "and", as:

- $\mathbb{P}(A_1,A_2,\cdots, A_n)$ read as "the probability of events $A_1$ and $A_2$ and ... and $A_n$ all happen."
- $\mathbb{P}(A\mid B_1, B_2, \cdots , B_n)$ read as "the conditional probability of event $A$ happens given events $B_1$ and $B_2$ and ... and $B_n$ all have happened."

:::tip Multiplication Rule

If events $A_1,A_2,\cdots , A_n$ all have positive probability, then:

$$
\mathbb{P}(A_1, A_2, \cdots, A_n)=\mathbb{P}(A_1) \mathbb{P}(A_2\mid a_1) \mathbb{P}(A_3\mid A_1, A_2)\cdots \mathbb{P}(A_n\mid A_1,\cdots, A_{n-1})
$$

</details>

:::

## Conditional Probability as Probability Function

:::tip Conditional Probability as Probability Function

Conditional Probability could be considered as a probability function as it fits all three [probability axioms](docs\Statistics\Probability-Theory\Probability-Basics.mdx#Probability-Axioms), and we define it as:
$$
\widetilde{P}(A)\overset{\text{def}}{=} \mathbb{P}(A \mid B).
$$

<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>

1. Non-negativity: Because $O(A\cap B) \ge 0$, therefore: 

$$
\widetilde{P}(A)=\mathbb{P}(A \mid B)=\frac{\mathbb{P}(A\cap B)}{\mathbb{P}(B)}\ge 0
$$

2. Normalization: Because $B\subset \Omega$ we know that $B \cap \Omega =B$, therefore:

$$
\widetilde{P}(\Omega)=\mathbb{P}(\Omega\mid B)=\frac{\mathbb{P}(\Omega\cap B)}{\mathbb{P}(B)}=\frac{\mathbb{P}(B)}{\mathbb{P}(B)}=1
$$

3. Countable Additivity: Let $A_1,A_2,\cdots $ be disjoint sets. Because $A_i\cap B \subseteq A_i$, the countable collection of sets $A_1\cap B$, $A_2\cap B$, \cdots must also be disjoint, therefore: 

$$
\begin{align*}
    \widetilde{P}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} A_i\right)&=\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\mid B \right) &&\text{(Definition of Conditional Probability)}\\
                                                        &=\frac{\mathbb{P}((\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}A_i)\cap B)}{\mathbb{P}(B)} &&\text{(Distributive Property)}\\
                                                        &=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(A_1\cap B)}{\mathbb{P}(B)} &&\text{($P$ Satisfies Axiom 3)}\\
                                                        &=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(A_i\cap  B)}{\mathbb{P}(B)} &&\text{(Distribute)}\\
                                                        &=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(A_i\mid B) &&\text{(Definition of Conditional Probability)}\\
                                                        &=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\widetilde{P}(A_i)
\end{align*}
$$

As $\widetilde{P}(A)$ satisfies all three probability axioms. Hence, we can say that Conditional Probability could be considered as a probability function. 

$$\blacksquare$$

</details>
:::
