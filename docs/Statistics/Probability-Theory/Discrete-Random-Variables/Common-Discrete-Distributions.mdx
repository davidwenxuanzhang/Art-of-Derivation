##  Bernoulli Distribution

::::tip Bernoulli Distribution

If $X$ is a random variable with the PMF


$$
p_X(x) = 
\left\{\begin{aligned}
&p, &x=1\\
&1-p, &x=0
\end{aligned}
\right.
$$

for some $0\le p\le 1$, $X$ is said to be a **Bernoulli random variable with parameter $p$**, written as $X\sim \operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)$
:::info Example
The Bernoulli distribution models a single trial with two possible outcomes, such as success or fail, true or false, yes or no.
:::


::::

:::tip Bernoulli Expectation and Variance

Let $X\sim \operatorname{Bernoulli}(p) $, then:
$$
\mathbb{E}[X]=p
$$
and 
$$
\operatorname{Var}[X]=p(1-p)
$$

## Binomial Distribution
::::tip Binomial Distribution

If $X$ is a random variable with the PMF


$$
p_X(x)=\binom{n}{x}p^x(1-p)^{n-x}; \qquad x=0,1,\dots ,n
$$

for some $n$ and $0\le p\le 1$, $X$ is said to be a **Binomial random variable with parameters $n$ and $p$**, written as  $X\sim \operatorname{Binomial}(n, p)$

:::tip Binomial Expectation and Variance

Let $X\sim \operatorname{Binomial}(p) $, then:
$$
\mathbb{E}[X]=np
$$
and 
$$
\operatorname{Var}[X]=np(1-p)
$$
<details>
<summary><span className="math-keyword">Derivation</span></summary>


Given a Binomial random variable $X\sim \operatorname{Binomial}(p) $, we have the PMF: 

$$
p_X(x)=\mathbb{P}(x=k)=\binom{n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k} 
$$
<hr />
 
- Expected Value:

Use the definition of Expected Value:

$$
\begin{align*}
    \mathbb{E}[X]&=\sum_{k}k\cdot p_X(k)\\
                 &=\sum_{k}k\binom{n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k} \\
                 &=\sum_{k}n\binom{n-1}{k-1}p^k(1-p)^{n-k} &&(\text{“Choose a Captain” identity})\\
                 &=np\sum_{k}\binom{n-1}{k-1}p^{k-1}(1-p)^{n-k}\\
\end{align*}
$$

Let $j=k-1 \implies k=j+1,\ n-k=n-(j+1)=n-1-j$
$$
\begin{align*}
    \implies \mathbb{E}[X]&=np\sum_{j}\underbrace{\binom{n-1}{j}p^j(1-p)^{n-1-j}}_{\text{PMF of }Y \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(n-1,p)} &&\text{(PMF sum up to 1)}\\
                          &=np
\end{align*}
$$
<hr />
- Variance:

Use the definition of Variance:


$$
\operatorname{Var}[X]=\mathbb{E}[X^2]-\left(\mathbb{E}[X] \right)^2=\mathbb{E}[X^2]-(np)^2
$$
Let $g(x)=x^2$, using LotUS:

$$
\begin{align*}
    \implies \mathbb{E}[X^2]&= \sum_{k}g(k)\cdot p_X(k)\\
                            &= \sum_{k}k^2\binom{n}{k}p^{k}(1-p)^{n-k}\\
                            &= \sum_{k}kn\binom{n-1}{k-1}p^{k}(1-p)^{n-k} &&(\text{“Choose a Captain” identity})\\
                            &=np\sum_{k=1}k\binom{n-1}{k-1}p^{k-1}(1-p)^{n-k}
\end{align*}
$$
Let $j=k-1 \implies k=j+1,\ n-k=n-(j+1)=n-1-j$
$$
\begin{align*}
    \implies \mathbb{E}[X^2]&=np\sum_{j=0}(j+1)\underbrace{\binom{n-1}{j}p^{j}(1-p)^{n-1-j}}_{\text{PMF of }Y\sim \operatorname {Binomial}(n-1,p)}\\
                            &=np\left(\underbrace{\sum_{j=0}j\binom{n-1}{j}p^{j}(1-p)^{n-1-j}}_{\mathbb{E}[Y]} + \underbrace{\sum_{j=0}\binom{n-1}{j}p^{j}(1-p)^{n-1-j}}_{1} \right)\\
                            &=np\left[(n-1)p\right]+1\\
                            &=(np)^2-np^2+np
\end{align*}
$$
Plug in back to Variance:
$$
\operatorname{Var}[X]=\mathbb{E}[X^2]-(np)^2= (np)^2-np^2+np-(np)^2=np-np^2=np(1-p) \tag*{$\blacksquare$}

$$
</details>
